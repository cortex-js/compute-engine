A function that a function body defines, and that the body assigns by its name to an outer variable, does not see the variables of the body when it is called after the call has ended.

## Witness

At `0aa4a0a4d0c9f2b53ab6924a111046541807193a`, with `evaluate()` and with
`evaluateAsync()`:

```ts
ce.declare('g', 'function');
ce.assign('g', ce.box(['Function', 'x', 'x']));
ce.box([
  ['Function',
    ['Block',
      ['Declare', 'c', 'real', 5],
      ['DefineFunction', 'h', ['Function', ['Add', 'c', 'k'], 'k']],
      ['Assign', 'g', 'h']],
    'n'],
  1,
]).evaluate();
ce.box('g').evaluate().json;         // "h"
ce.box(['g', 10]).evaluate().json;   // ["Add", "c", 10]   (must be 15)
```

When the body returns `h` instead, the result is the function literal, and
`g(10)` gives `15`.

## Cause and options

`Assign(g, h)` stores the symbol `h`, not a closure. After the call, the call
of `h` reads `c` through the body scope of the literal, whose parent is the
call frame only while the call runs, and no closure that holds the frame is in
the chain of the caller (`capturedRunOf()`, `src/compute-engine/
function-utils.ts`). A returned `h` is replaced by its literal, closed over
the run (`resolveEscapingLambda()`).

Fix: when the value of an `Assign` to an outer variable is the name of a
function that the body defines, store its literal closed over the run, as
`resolveEscapingLambda()` does for a returned name, and re-rooted on the call
frame (`rerootOnCreatingFrame()`). Size S.
