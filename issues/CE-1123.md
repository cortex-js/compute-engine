An integral with one integral sign and two differentials, `\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x}\,dy`, reads the first `dx` as the product of a variable `d` and `x`, and gives a value that holds `d`.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-07, on `90185c2d020a26dcaf4a084f264dce55d8bcee57` with
the CE-1045 change:

```
\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x}\,dy           Integrate(Divide(d·x, x), Tuple(y, 1, 2)); evaluate() gives d
\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x}\cdot 2\,dy    evaluate() gives 2d
\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x}\frac{dy}{y}   evaluate() gives d ln 2
\iint_1^2 \frac{dx}{x}\frac{dy}{y}  unexpected-differential error (CE-1045)
```

The expression is valid, and no diagnostic tells that `d` is not a
differential.

## Cause and options

`parseIntegral()` in `latex-syntax/dictionary/definitions-calculus.ts`
calls `markMisplacedDifferentials()` only when the integral found fewer
differentials than it has integral signs. With one sign and the differential
`dy` found, the `dx` inside the fraction stays the product `d·x`.

Options: (a) also mark a differential in the body when the integral found
all its differentials, as an `unexpected-differential` error; the risk is a
body where `d` is a real variable (`\int \frac{d x}{x}\,dy` with a declared
`d`). (b) Keep the parse, and add a diagnostic for a `d` followed by a
variable in a body that has its differential. (c) Close: an integral with
more differentials than signs is not well formed, and reading `d` as a
variable is defensible.
