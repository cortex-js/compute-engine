A drawing that colors a list of points by their index, with a range that ends at the length of a computed list, opens and moves its sliders about two times slower, because the whole list is computed while the formula is read.

## Witness

CE 0.198.1 and 0.200.0. A prepared Document with the inputs `w` and `Z`
declared `real`, and the rows:

```
q(x, y) = Sin(12.9898x + 78.233y + w) - Floor(Sin(12.9898x + 78.233y + w))
L = Map(Function(Sin(kZ) + Cos(kw) + k, k), Range(1, 1029))
P = Filter(L, Function(k < 0.5, k))
c = <case>
```

Median of five prepares, each on a new engine (CE 0.200.0):

- `c = q(0, Range(1, Length(P)))`: 1952 ms.
- `c = q(0, Range(1, 1029))` (control): 6.8 ms.
- `c = Length(P)` (control): 6.7 ms.

A CPU profile of the case (minified bundle, Tycho's product route) shows
the chain: the prepare boxes row `c` → canonical form of the call of `q` →
`isFiniteCollection` of the argument `Range(1, Length(P))` → `count` of the
range → evaluation of `Length(P)` → evaluation of every element of `P`
(`_memoizedLazyCollectionValue`). The values are symbolic in `w` and `Z`,
so each element is evaluated symbolically. Expected: the canonical form
does not evaluate a range bound; an unknown count leaves the finiteness
unknown, as it does for `c = Length(P)`.

In Tycho, `art/wccrhnzn2q` has the row
`c_p = hsv(q(0, 1..Length(P_1)) × 360, 0.8, 0.9)` over a list of 1029
points. In the CE Document port, its registration takes 199 ms instead of
78 ms, its open 596 ms instead of 259 ms, and three sliders about 90 ms
instead of 48 ms (nine paired rounds, 2026-11-23). Without the row, the
document registers in 24 ms. The probe time grows with the cost of one
element, so a longer list or a costlier element takes seconds.

The instrument prints `CE-1723: READY` when the case prepares in at most
3 times the first control.

## Cause and options

Not investigated on the CE side beyond the profile above. The query that
the canonical form of the call makes (whether the argument is a finite
collection, for the broadcast of `q`) reads the count of the range, and
the count evaluates its upper bound.

## CE answer

Fixed. The broadcast typing and the argument check of a function literal
read the count of an operand before its type. They now test the type first
(`isLambdaBroadcastParticipant` and `lambdaCallOperandOfDescriptor` in
`boxed-function.ts`, `tupleBroadcastArityOnViews`, the arm-1 filter of
`broadcastLiftType` and `viewOfDescriptor` in `broadcast-lift-type.ts`,
`liftedOperandCannotFit` in `validate.ts`). The witness prepares the case in
14.5 ms against 10.4 ms for the control (it was 4593 ms on this machine).
Nested rows (`360·q(0, Range(1, Length(P)))`, `Sin(…) + 1`) compute no
element of `P` in the prepare either. `Norm(Range(1, Length(P)))` still
counts the range in its own type handler: CE-1754.
