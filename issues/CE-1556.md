Counting, reading or transforming the characters of a string, or the elements of a list of strings, does not compile.

## Witness

On javascript, with `s: string` and `L: list<string>`:
`Count(Characters(s), c -> c == "a")`, `At(Characters(s), 1)`,
`Map(L, ToUpperCase)`, `Sort(L)` and `Reverse(Characters(s))` decline
("a list of strings other than a literal, Characters, an input read by
StringJoin, or the result"), and run on the interpreter. Not measured
against 0.193.0.

## Cause and options

CE-1548 admits a list of strings at the boundary only (`stringListRefusal()`
of `compilation/ir/index.ts`): made by a literal or `Characters`, read by
`StringJoin`, or returned. A list of run-time length is never written out by
the passes, so each read of an element survives and refuses. Admitting the
reads needs, for each list path: an absent string for `At` out of range
(`_at` gives `NaN`), `Equal` of strings (no row; the conditioning of both
operands), the code-point order of `Sort` and the equality of `Unique`, and
the element rule of `Map`, `Filter` and the folds. The user chose to keep
them declined in CE-1548 (2026-11-19); this issue is the work when wanted.
