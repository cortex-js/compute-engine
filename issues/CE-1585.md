At a high precision, the harmonic number of a complex number shows many more digits than are correct.

## Witness

With `ce.precision = 50`,
`ce.function('HarmonicNumber', [ce.number(ce.complex(-2, 1e-20))]).N()` gives
`["Complex", {"num": "0.99999999999999876060651209008240243104215933593992"}, 100000000000000020000]`
with the CE-1572 fix. The real part is shown with 50 digits; only the first 15
are right. The value is about `1 + 2.0e-41 + 1e20i` (mpmath at 60 digits,
`psi(0, n + 1) + euler`). The same digits come from
`Add(Digamma(-1 + 1e-20i), EulerGamma).N()`.

## Cause and options

The complex `Digamma` is a machine kernel. Its value is added to `γ` at the
working precision, and the sum keeps the 50 digits of `γ`, so the digits past
the 16th are those of `γ`, not of `ψ`. Options: (a) round a complex sum with
a machine operand to machine precision; (b) give `HarmonicNumber` of a complex
n a machine result when its kernels are machine (`H(n) = H(n + 1) − 1/(n + 1)`
near a negative integer also removes the cancellation of the real part).
Size S.
