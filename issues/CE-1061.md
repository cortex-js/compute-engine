`simplify()` leaves some infinite series as written, while `evaluate()` gives their closed forms.

## Witness

```
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k^2}    simplify: the sum   evaluate: π²/6
\sum_{k=0}^\infty x^k              simplify: the sum   evaluate: If(|x| < 1, 1/(1 − x))
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{x^k}{k}    simplify: the sum   evaluate: If(|x| < 1, −ln(1 − x))
```

## Cause and options

`simplify()` of a `Sum` gives the closed form that `evaluate()` gives only for
the telescoping sums (`bigOpClosedForm()` in `library/utils.ts`). The other
closed forms of `evaluate()` (`infiniteSumClosedForm()`, and the geometric and
named series of `symbolicSumClosedForm()`) call `.simplify()` on their result
(`geometricValue()`, `namedSeriesClosedForm()`), and a simplification rule must
not call `.simplify()` (`CODING-STYLEGUIDE.md`, "Engine implementation").

Option: remove the `.simplify()` calls from these closed forms, give the
canonical form of the result instead (the cost of `simplify()` then reduces
it in the outer loop: `1/(1 − 1/√2)` to `2 + √2`), and let `bigOpClosedForm()`
call them. Check that `evaluate()` still gives the readable form for a radical
ratio (`Σ (1/√2)^k`), which is why `geometricValue()` calls `.simplify()`.
