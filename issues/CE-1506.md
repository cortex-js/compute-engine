A domain-colouring shader of the tangent of a complex number shows infinities next to its poles, where the values are finite.

## Witness

On GLSL, `Tan(z)` with `z: complex` is the helper `_gpu_ir_ctan`:
`vec2(sin 2x, sinh 2y) / (cos 2x + cosh 2y)`. Next to a pole
(`z = π/2 - 0.0001 + 0.0001i`), `cos 2x` is about `-1` and `cosh 2y` about
`1`, and in float32 their sum cancels to 0, so the helper gives infinities;
the interpreter gives about `5000 + 5000i`. `_gpu_ir_ctanh`
(`cosh 2x + cos 2y`) cancels the same way next to its poles. `_gpu_ir_ccot`
had the same form and was rewritten in CE-1468 (2026-11-19).

## Cause and options

The denominators subtract nearly equal numbers. The stable forms divide by
a sum of squares: `tan z = (sin x cos x, sinh y cosh y) / (cos²x + sinh²y)`
and `tanh z = (sinh x cosh x, sin y cos y) / (sinh²x + cos²y)`, as
`_gpu_ir_ccot` now does. Check each against the interpreter at a few
points, and add a point next to a pole to the WebGL value test of
`test/compute-engine/ir-glsl-complex.test.ts`.
