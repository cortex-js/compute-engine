A textbook integral of 1/(x·ln^1.1 x) up to infinity gives a value with an error bar wider than the value.

## Witness

```
\int_{1/2}^{1} \frac{dt}{(1-t)(-\ln(1-t))^{1.1}}   .N() gives 3 ± 36 (2026-11-06), exact 10.3733
\int_2^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x(\ln x)^{1.1}}           same situation
\int_0^{1/2} \frac{dt}{t(-\ln t)^{1.1}}             gives 10.37329 ± 0.00007 (correct)
```

## Cause and options

Next to a bound at `0`, the part that cannot be sampled comes from a model
fitted to about 1000 shells (`shellTail`, `numerics/endpoint-quadrature.ts`).
Next to a nonzero or infinite bound a corner has fewer than 30 accurate
shells, and a model needs 64, so the value stays the plain quadrature; only
the error bar is correct. Fix: in `adaptiveQuadrature`, a change of variable
that moves the singular bound to `0` (the same change as CE-182). The
too-small error of `x^{-0.9999}` is CE-966.
