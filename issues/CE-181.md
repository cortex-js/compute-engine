An integral with a logarithmic singularity at the bound 1 gives a value with an error bar wider than the value.

## Witness

```
\int_{1/2}^{1} \frac{dt}{(1-t)(-\ln(1-t))^{1.1}}   .N() gives 3 ± 36, exact 10.3733
\int_{1/2}^{1} \frac{-1}{(1-t)\ln(1-t)}dt          .N() gives 3.97 ± 71637, exact +∞
\int_0^{1/2} \frac{dt}{t(-\ln t)^{1.1}}             gives 10.37329 ± 0.00007 (correct)
```

Readings of 2026-11-07. The same integrals over `[2, ∞)` give
`10.37326 ± 0.00017` and `+∞` since the CE-182 change.

## Cause and options

Next to a bound at `0`, the part that cannot be sampled comes from a model
fitted to about 1000 shells (`shellTail`, `numerics/endpoint-quadrature.ts`).
Next to a nonzero bound a corner has fewer than 30 accurate shells, and a
model needs 64, so the value stays the plain quadrature; only the error bar
is correct. The integrand computes `1 − t` in doubles, so no numeric change
of variable gives more shells: a fix must substitute `u = 1 − t` in the
expression before it is evaluated. The too-small error of `x^{-0.9999}` is
CE-966.
