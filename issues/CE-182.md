The divergent integral of 1/(x·ln x) from 2 to infinity gives a finite number.

## Witness

```
NIntegrate(x ↦ 1/(x ln x), 2, +∞)               gives 3.9655141955491193, should be +∞
\int_2^\infty \frac{dx}{x\ln x}  .N()           gives 3.97 ± 71060
\int_{1/2}^{1} \frac{-1}{(1-t)\ln(1-t)}dt .N()  gave 4.0 ± 1.7
```

`evaluate()` gives `+∞` for both integrals. Readings of 2026-11-06, except
the last.

## Cause and options

Next to `0` the quadrature bisects about 1070 dyadic shells, enough for the
power-law test (`shellsDecayAsPower`, `numerics/gauss-kronrod.ts`) to read the
exponent of `1/k` shells as `1`. Next to a nonzero bound, or at infinity after
the change of variable, the corner panel bisects only about 48 times; there
the exponent is `0.87` for this divergent integrand and `0.95` for the
convergent `1/(x ln^{1.1} x)`, so no threshold separates them. Fix: a change
of variable that moves the bound to `0` (see CE-181). The sign of a detected
divergence is CE-967.
