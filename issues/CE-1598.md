ζ(0, a) typed in LaTeX stays unevaluated, while the same function written as HurwitzZeta(0, a) gives 1/2 − a.

## Witness

`\zeta(s,a)` parses as the two-argument `Zeta`. On 0.194.3 `\zeta(0,a)`
evaluates to `Zeta(0, a)` and `\zeta(-1,a)` to `Zeta(-1, a)`, while
`HurwitzZeta(0, a)` gives `1/2 - a` and `HurwitzZeta(-1, a)` gives
`-a^2/2 + a/2 - 1/12`. Controls: `HurwitzZeta(0, a)` as above, and the numeric
`\zeta(0, 1/3)` gives `1/6`.

## Cause and options

The two-argument `Zeta` evaluates numerically like `HurwitzZeta` but does not
have its symbolic rules. Option: canonicalize `Zeta(s, a)` to
`HurwitzZeta(s, a)`, or make the two share one evaluate handler. Size S.

## Resolution

`Zeta(0, a)` with a symbolic `a` now evaluates to `1/2 - a`: at s = 0 the
two-argument `Zeta` and `HurwitzZeta` agree at every base point. The issue's
option to canonicalize `Zeta(s, a)` to `HurwitzZeta(s, a)` is not sound: at a
negative base point the two differ (`Zeta(-1, -5/2)` is `109/24`,
`HurwitzZeta(-1, -5/2)` is `-107/24`). Thus `\zeta(-1, a)` stays unevaluated
unless `a` is known to be positive; with `a > 0` assumed it gives
`-a^2/2 + a/2 - 1/12`.
