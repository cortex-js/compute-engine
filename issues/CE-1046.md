Two integral signs with both differentials in the numerator of a fraction give a wrong value when a term follows the inner integral or a factor comes before it.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-07, after the CE-1031 fix:

```
\int_1^2\int_3^4 \frac{dx\,dy}{xy} + 1     evaluate() gives 1 - 2ln(2)ln(3) - 2ln(2) + 4ln(2)^2 + ln(3)
\int_1^2\int_3^4 \frac{1}{xy}\,dx\,dy + 1  evaluate() gives 1 + 2ln(2)^2 - ln(2)ln(3)   (correct)
\int_1^2 2\int_3^4 \frac{dx\,dy}{xy}       evaluate() gives -4ln(2)ln(3) - 4ln(2) + 2ln(3) + 8ln(2)^2
\int_1^2 2\int_3^4 \frac{1}{xy}\,dx\,dy    evaluate() gives -2ln(2)ln(3) + 4ln(2)^2   (correct)
```

## Cause and options

An integral with more differentials in its integrand than signs records its
parts (`excessDifferentials` in
`latex-syntax/dictionary/definitions-calculus.ts`). An enclosing integral
takes the extra differentials only when that integral is its whole body. With
`+ 1`, the `splitSumAtDifferential()` branch of `parseIntegral()` returns
`Add(Integrate(...), 1)` and records nothing. With the factor `2`, the body is
`InvisibleOperator(2, Integrate(...))`. The outer integral has no variable.

Options: (a) record the parts in the sum branch too, rebuild the integral
term, and keep the terms after it outside both integrals, as the token form
does; search the last factor of a product in the same way. (b) Leave as is.
Recommendation: (a), size S.
