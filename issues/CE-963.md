A numeric integral next to a singular bound reports an error about 6,000 times larger than the error of its quadrature.

## Witness

```
N(Integrate(t ↦ cos(t/2)/√(cos t − cos ½), [0, ½]))
  reports ± 7.0e-4 (= 2.22/√10⁷); the quadrature error is 1.2e-7
```

## Cause and options

A quadrature result that did not converge and is next to a singular bound
(`singularCorner`) reports at least `|estimate|/√mcSamples`
(`integrateRealPart()`, `library/calculus.ts`). The floor exists because the
error of a result that did not converge can be far too small. The quadrature
error already covers the difference with tanh-sinh and the uncertainty of the
corners.

Question: keep the floor for a `singularCorner` result?

- (a) Keep it. Nothing changes.
- (b) Do not apply it to a `singularCorner` result, after a measurement that
  shows that its error covers the true error on the endpoint test corpus. The
  integral above becomes `± 1.2e-7`. Cost: the measurement and one condition.

Recommended: (b), only if the measurement passes; otherwise (a). If nothing
is decided, (a).
