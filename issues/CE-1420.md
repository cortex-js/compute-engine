A hexadecimal numeral that starts with `0b`, such as `"0bad"_{16}`, is read as a binary numeral.

## Witness

Main 8a61d477c: `\text{0b1}_{16}` and `["Subscript", "'0b1'", 16]` give 1;
the value is 177 (0xb1). `["Subscript", "'0x10'", 10]` gives 16, where
`x` is not a digit of base 10 (an `unexpected-digit` error is expected).
After CE-1401, `DigitsFrom("- 0b1", 16)`, `"-+0b1"` and `"--0b1"` in base 16
still give -1 or 1 (crafted: `fromDigits` strips any number of signs and
spaces before it reads the prefix).

## Cause and options

`fromDigits` (`src/compute-engine/numerics/strings.ts`, around line 213)
reads a lowercase `0x` or `0b` as a radix prefix and lets it override the
base it is handed. Its callers are the base-form `Subscript` handler
(`library/core.ts`, around line 10925), `DigitsFrom` and `NumberFrom`.
`NumberFrom` rejects such a numeral before the call; CE-1401 makes
`DigitsFrom` drop the leading `0` of a numeral whose prefix does not match the
base. Fix: in `fromDigits`, read the prefix only when no base is given or when
the base is the prefix's (16 for `0x`, 2 for `0b`); then the two work-arounds
in `DigitsFrom` and `NumberFrom` can go. Size S.
