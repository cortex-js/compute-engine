Boxing, simplifying and integrating are up to 4.3 times slower than in version 0.118.2 (measured 2026-11-06 on 0.182.0).

## Witness

`∫₁² 1/x dx` takes 194 µs on 0.182.0 against 45 µs on 0.118.2 (4.3 times);
boxing `√6x + √2x` takes 1.46 times as long. The measures, the profiles and the
older readings are in `docs/plans/2026-11-07-ce-351-slowdown-measures.md`.

## Cause and options

Two commits gave box 18 %, simplify 9 % and the definite integral 8 to 11 %
(the `Multiply` scalar case; one lift of the integrand). What remains: the
definite integral spends about 30 % of its time boxing and applying the
function literal of `EvaluateAt`, and 10 % in the antiderivative. The `Add`
type handler takes about 2 % of boxing the witness, whose operands are typed
`number`: a scalar case there does not help. Keep
literal-tier types (decision 2026-09-06). Plan:
`docs/plans/2026-09-06-type-facts-per-type.md`.

A third commit (2026-11-07) made box 10 % faster and the other cases 0 to
5 %. The profiles are now flat (no function above 4 % on its own); what
remains is listed in the measures document. The next step is a change of
design, not a local shortcut: for example, effects of an inline function
literal without its full type, or a cheaper application of a literal in
`EvaluateAt`.

A fourth commit (2026-11-08) computes the effects of an inline function
literal without its full type when the type is not in its cache:
`∫₁² 1/x dx` is 11 to 12 % faster, the other cases do not change. The
types are computed again during one evaluation because each literal that
is boxed moves the type version (measures document, "Measures of
2026-11-08"); a change of that rule needs its own analysis of soundness.
