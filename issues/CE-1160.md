An integral with its differential in the numerator of a fraction, a factor or a minus sign before it and a term after it, such as `2\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1+x^2} + 1`, gives a wrong value: the factor or the sign is also applied to the term after it.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-08, on `01761c21599a0ea80e443afa1b3a6d39d752fc89`:

```
2\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1+x^2} + 1       evaluate() gives 2(1 + π/4) = 3.5708
2\int_0^1 \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx + 1    evaluate() gives 1 + π/2 = 2.5708   (correct)
-\int_3^4 \frac{dx}{x} + 5           evaluate() gives -5 - 2ln(2) + ln(3) = -5.2877
-\int_3^4 \frac{1}{x}\,dx + 5        evaluate() gives 5 - 2ln(2) + ln(3) = 4.7123   (correct)
1-\int_3^4 \frac{dx}{x} + 5          evaluate() gives -4 - 2ln(2) + ln(3) = -4.2877
1-\int_3^4 \frac{1}{x}\,dx + 5       evaluate() gives 6 - 2ln(2) + ln(3) = 5.7123   (correct)
```

The raw parses of the wrong lines are
`InvisibleOperator(2, Add(Integrate(…), 1))`,
`Negate(Add(Integrate(…), 5))` and `Subtract(1, Add(Integrate(…), 5))`.
Without a factor or a sign before the integral, the value is correct:
`\int_3^4 \frac{dx}{x} + 5` is `Add(Integrate(…), 5)`.

## Cause and options

`parseIntegral()` in `latex-syntax/dictionary/definitions-calculus.ts` reads
the body of the integral with a precedence lower than that of addition, so
the body holds the terms after the integrand. When the differential is after
the integrand (`\frac{1}{x}\,dx`), the body stops at the differential, and
the parser of the enclosing expression reads the terms after it. When the
differential is inside a term (`\frac{dx}{x}`), the body is the whole sum:
`splitSumAtDifferential()` finds the terms after the term that holds the
differential, and `finishIntegral()` returns `Add(Integrate(…), terms…)` as
the result of the `\int` command. The parser of the factor, of the minus
sign or of the subtraction that is before the integral then takes this sum
as its operand.

When an enclosing integral takes extra differentials of this integral
(`\int_1^2 2\int_3^4 \frac{dx\,dy}{xy} + 1`), `claimExcessDifferentials()`
and `claimExcessDifferentialsInSum()` put the terms after the enclosing
integral, so that case is correct.

A brace group is not kept in the parsed expression, so in that case
`1-{\int_3^4 \frac{dx\,dy}{xy}+5}` gives the same parse as the spelling
without braces: 5 is added after the enclosing integral, although the group
subtracts it (the same for `-{…+5}` and `2{…+5}`; `crafted`). A fix that
keeps the trailing terms out of the result of the `\int` command also
corrects these spellings.

Options, none tried:

- (a) `parseIntegral()` returns only the integral, and sets the position of
  the parser back to the `+` or `-` that ends the integrand, so that the
  enclosing parser reads the terms. The position is known today only from a
  scan of the tokens (`integrandEndToken()`), which gives a wrong position
  for a sum in braces (CE-1077), and setting the position back discards the
  diagnostics of the tokens read after it.
- (b) `parseIntegralBody()` reads the body one term at a time, and stops at
  the first `+` or `-` after a term that holds a differential. No position is
  set back. The search for a differential in a term
  (`splitSumAtDifferential()`) then runs once for each term.
- (c) Leave as is.

Recommendation: (b).
