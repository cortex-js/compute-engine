With the extra integration rules loaded, the upper tail integral of `y^{3/2} e^{-y/2}` from `x` to infinity takes about 1.1 to 1.4 s, while the same integral to 10 takes about 30 ms.

## Witness

Measured on commit `40ba059a8444959fbcfb94fe393d1a540d423088`, on a loaded
machine, after `loadIntegrationRules(ce)` (`src/integration-rules`):

| Program | Time |
| --- | --- |
| `\int y^{3/2} e^{-y/2} dy` `.evaluate()` | about 75 ms (`3√2 √π Erf(√y/√2) - 6 e^{-y/2} √y - …`) |
| `\int_x^\infty y^{3/2} e^{-y/2} dy` `.evaluate()` | about 1.4 s, then 1.1 s the second time |
| `\int_x^{10} y^{3/2} e^{-y/2} dy` `.evaluate()` | about 30 ms |
| the integral to ∞ without the rules | about 15 ms (unevaluated) |

The result to ∞ is right; the time goes to the endpoint at ∞, not to the
antiderivative. Test:
`test/compute-engine/integration-rules-substitutions.test.ts`.

## Cause and options

Not profiled. The antiderivative has `Erf(√y/√2)` and `e^{-y/2} y^{3/2}`
terms; the limit of each at `y → ∞` is the likely cost. An agent profiles the
evaluation at the ∞ endpoint first, then either caches the limit of each term
or adds the limits `Erf(u) → 1` and `e^{-u} u^k → 0` for `u → ∞` before the
general limit search. Size S once the cause is known.
