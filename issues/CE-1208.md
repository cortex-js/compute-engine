The natural logarithm of a complex number whose real part is a perfect square evaluates to the logarithm of the real part alone.

## Witness

At `d221dfaf6e34746686e2a82d32b1d055991ca73d`:

| Program | `evaluate()` | `N()` of that | `N()` of the program |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Ln(Complex(9, 9))` | `2·Ln(3)` | `2.1972245773362196` | `2.543798167616192 + 0.7853981633974483i` |
| `Ln(Complex(4, 4))` | `2·Ln(2)` | `1.3862943611198906` | `1.7328679513998633 + 0.7853981633974483i` |
| `Ln(Complex(9, 3))` | `2·Ln(3)` | `2.1972245773362196` | `2.2499048351651325 + 0.3217505543966422i` |
| `Ln(Complex(1, 1))`, `Ln(Complex(2, 2))`, `Ln(Complex(3, 3))` | unchanged | right | right |

`N()` of the program is right; `evaluate()` drops the imaginary part when the
real part is the square of an integer. Compiled code gives the value of `N()`.
The random tester met it as `Ln(z·w·x)` at `z = 1 + i`, `w = 3`, `x = 3`.

## Cause and options

Not investigated. A rule of `Ln` that rewrites the logarithm of a perfect
power (`Ln(9)` as `2·Ln(3)`) reads the real part of its operand and does not
test that the operand is real. Option: apply the rule only to a real operand.
