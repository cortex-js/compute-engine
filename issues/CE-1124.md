Two integral signs with both differentials in the numerator of a fraction give a wrong value when a term comes before the inner integral.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-07, with the CE-1046 fix:

```
\int_1^2 1+\int_3^4 \frac{dx\,dy}{xy}      evaluate() gives 1 - 2ln(2)ln(3) - 2ln(2) + 4ln(2)^2 + ln(3)
\int_1^2 1+\int_3^4 \frac{1}{xy}\,dx\,dy   evaluate() gives 1 + 2ln(2)^2 - ln(2)ln(3)   (correct)
\int_1^2 1-\int_3^4 \frac{dx\,dy}{xy}      evaluate() gives 1 + 2ln(2)ln(3) - 4ln(2)^2 + 2ln(2) - ln(3)
```

The raw parse of the first line is
`Integrate(Add(1, Integrate(1/(xy), Tuple(y, 3, 4), x)), Tuple(Nothing, 1, 2))`:
the outer integral has no variable.

## Cause and options

The inner integral records its extra differentials (`excessDifferentials` in
`latex-syntax/dictionary/definitions-calculus.ts`).
`claimExcessDifferentials()` finds the inner integral when it is the body of
the outer integral, the last factor of a product, or the operand of a
negation, but not when it is the last term of a sum or a difference. The
inner integral parses every term after it, so it is always the last operand
of the outer body.

Options: (a) in `parseIntegral()`, when the body is a sum or a difference and
`splitSumAtDifferential()` finds no differential in its terms, search the last
operand with `claimExcessDifferentials()` and rebuild the sum (`Subtract(1, I)`
as for a negation). The search must come after `splitSumAtDifferential()`, so
that a differential in an earlier term (`\int \frac{dy}{y} + \int ...`) is not
taken by mistake. (b) Leave as is. Recommendation: (a), size S.
