An integral with no variable written, of a formula that holds another integral whose formula reads a variable that holds a formula, works with respect to the variable of the inner integral.

## Witness

With `G := sin(x)` and `x`, `y` with no value:

```
\int_0^1\int_0^1 G y        y − y·cos(1)     (must be (1 − cos(1))/2 ≈ 0.2298)
\int_0^1\int_0^1 \sin(x) y  1/2 − cos(1)/2   (the formula written in place)
```

The canonical form of the first is
`Integrate(Function(Integrate(Function(G·y, x), Limits(Nothing, 0, 1)), x), Limits(Nothing, 0, 1))`:
the two integrals have the variable `x`. Before the fix of CE-1787 the value
was `1/4` (the variables were `G` and `y`).

## Cause and options

The candidates of the implicit variable (`implicitVariableCandidates()`,
`boxed-expression/implicit-variable-rule.ts`) are read from the set of the
symbols that the body reads (`references`), with no position. `G` is read
inside the inner integral, which binds `x`, and the formula of `G` is read
in that `x`. The outer integral sees `G` as a free symbol and takes `x`, a
candidate of its formula, although that `x` is bound by the inner integral.

- (a) The candidates of the formula of a symbol do not include a name that a
  binder of a formula (`Integrate`, `Limit`, the literal of `ND`) binds
  around every occurrence of the symbol. This needs a walk that knows the
  binders around each occurrence (`rewriteWithBinders()`), in the canonical
  form and in the free-variable walk (`implicitCandidatesInWalk()`,
  `abstract-boxed-expression.ts`), which reads sets today.
- (b) Document that the variable of an outer integral must be written when
  an inner integral reads a symbol that holds a formula.
