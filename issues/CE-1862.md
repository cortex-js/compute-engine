The inverse of a singular matrix of complex decimals that has zeros in a column gives huge numbers instead of saying that the matrix has no inverse.

## Witness

At commit `9cc9aeef2eb908e3444001d242a501521557ee17`. In each matrix, row 2
is a multiple of row 1.

```
Inverse([[0.1i, 0.3i, 0], [0.3i, 0.9i, 0], [0, 1, 1]])   // evaluate() and N()
  // entries of about 5.4e16 i
Inverse([[0.6i, 0.7i, 0], [4.2i, 4.9i, 0], [0, 1, 1]])   // evaluate() and N()
  // entries of about 1e16 i
```

Each must stay unevaluated, as the same matrices without `i` do
(`Inverse([[0.1, 0.3, 0], [0.3, 0.9, 0], [0, 1, 1]])`), and as the first
matrix does with `5` and `15` in place of the two zeros of column 3.

## Cause and options

`complexMatrixInverse()` refuses a pivot that is a residue of the rounding: a
cell whose modulus is at most `1024ε` times its running bound. A residue that
is not chosen as a pivot is still the factor that clears its column from its
row. The bound of each cell of that row then grows by the modulus of the
factor times the bound of the pivot row. In a cell that was 0, the new value
and the new bound are the same residue, so the cell passes the test and is a
pivot of a later column.

Option: add the bound of the factor (`bound[i][k]`, read before the row is
updated) times the bound of the pivot row, in place of the modulus of the
factor. The bound of the factor is at least its modulus, so the test only
refuses more cells. `AbstractTensor.inexactInverse()` in
`src/compute-engine/tensor/tensors.ts` does this (CE-1822): 336 of 5,544
singular matrices of this form gave huge entries with the modulus of the
factor, and none with its bound. `numberMatrixInverse()` sets a residue to 0
and does not have this defect.
