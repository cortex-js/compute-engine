The integral of an oscillating function over an infinite range with a symbolic frequency gives a formula with an infinity in it instead of staying unevaluated.

## Witness

At commit `ffcfe2b5d3d67556d2bb64fd7ae2a9ec5e084056`:

```
\int_0^\infty \cos(xy)\,dx    evaluate gives sin(+oo * y) / y, should stay unevaluated (the integral diverges)
\int_0^\infty \cos(2x)\,dx    evaluate keeps the integral; N gives NaN
```

With a number for the frequency the integral stays unevaluated, as it
should. With a symbol, the antiderivative `sin(xy)/y` is evaluated at
`x = +∞` as if the limit existed.

## Cause and options

The definite integral applies the fundamental theorem of calculus with an
infinite bound by substitution, with no limit, when the antiderivative has
a symbolic parameter. Options: (a) when a bound is infinite, take the limit
of the antiderivative at that bound, and keep the integral unevaluated when
the limit does not exist or is not known; (b) keep the integral unevaluated
when the value at an infinite bound holds an infinite number literal. (b) is
the smaller change. Size S.
