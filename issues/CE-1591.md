The antiderivative of (x²+1)² stays an unevaluated integral, while the same polynomial written out integrates.

## Witness

On 0.194.3, `Integrate((x^2+1)^2, x)`, `Integrate((x^2+1)^3, x)` and
`Integrate((x^2+x)^2, x)` evaluate to the `Integrate` expression. Controls:
`Integrate(x^4+2x^2+1, x)` gives `x^5/5 + 2x^3/3 + x`; `(x+1)^3` (linear base)
and `x(x^2+1)^2` (substitution) integrate. The instrument checks each result
by its derivative.

## Cause and options

The antiderivative rules handle a power of a linear polynomial and a
substitution, but not a positive integer power of a polynomial of degree 2
or more. Option: when the integrand is a polynomial in the variable (after
`expand`) and no other rule applies, expand and integrate term by term. A
degree cap keeps large powers from expanding. Size S.

The user's workaround is to expand by hand.
