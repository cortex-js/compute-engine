The sum or difference of two lists of points is computed point by point on the slow route instead of the compiled one.

## Witness

`P, Q: list<tuple<X, X>>` with X = `real | signed_infinity | nan`, compiled
to javascript, run at P = [(1,2),(3,4)], Q = [(10,10),(20,20)]:

| expression | 0.193.0 | 0.194.2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PointX(Add(P, Q))` | [11, 23] | declines: "Add(P, Q): lists of different lengths or ranks" |
| `PointX(Subtract(P, Q))` | [-9, -17] | declines: "Add(P, Negate(Q)): lists of different lengths or ranks" |
| `Add(P, Q)` | [[11,12],[23,24]] | declines (same message) |

`Add` of two `list<real>` of unknown length compiles on 0.194.2, so the
point case is the odd one. In Tycho, a computed collection over two
point-list rows (`(P_1 + P_2).x`) falls to the interpreter.

## Expected

The three forms compile, with the interpreter's value. Unequal lengths at
run time are unspecified in compiled code (the rule for `list<real>`).

## Instrument

`npx tsx scripts/repros/2026-11-19-ce1537-add-point-lists-compile-probe.mts`
in Tycho. `CE-1537: READY` when the three compile and give the values above.
READY on 0.193.0, NOT READY on 0.194.2.
