A numerical derivative or integral of a variable that holds a formula gives no number.

## Witness

At d476bcce3fe25ae621a30965bd60d0cf8ed3a322:

```
G := sin(x)
ND(Function(G, x), 1).N()           ND((x) => G, 1)   (must be cos(1) ≈ 0.5403)
ND(G, 1).N()                        ND(G, 1)
NIntegrate(Function(G, x), 0, 1).N()   NaN            (must be 1 − cos(1) ≈ 0.4597)
```

After CE-1375, `D(G, x)` is `cos(x)` and `Integrate(G, Limits(x, 0, 1))` is
`1 − cos(1)`: the symbolic operators read the value of `G`, and an
occurrence of `x` in it denotes their variable.

## Cause and options

`ND` and `NIntegrate` evaluate their function literal at sample points. The
value of `G` reads the global `x`, not the parameter of the literal, so each
sample is not a number.

- (a) Recommended. Apply the rule of CE-1375 to `ND`, `NIntegrate` and
  `NLimit`: replace each symbol of the body that holds a formula in the
  variable by its value before the sampling (`withFormulaValues` in
  `library/calculus.ts`), and write them in the rule of
  `docs/SCOPING-MODEL.md`, "The variable of a formula".
- (b) Keep them as they are, and document that only the symbolic operators
  read such a value.
