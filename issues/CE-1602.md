An imported Desmos document that sums over a list with equal bounds takes about 13 seconds to open instead of under one.

## Witness

`x`, `y` declared `real` and assigned 0.4 and 0.6; `P` a literal list of N
points; `D` = `Add(Power(Add(x, Negate(PointX(P))), 2), Power(Add(y,
Negate(PointY(P))), 2))` (the list of squared distances); `f` =
`Function(Block(Which(Equal(k, Min(D)), 1e9, True, k)), k)`. Then
`ce.box(['Apply', f, D]).evaluate()` against `ce.box(['Map', f, D]).evaluate()`:

| N | Apply | Map | evaluate() calls, Apply / Map |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 49 ms | 10 ms | 2,937 / 137 |
| 64 | 193 ms | 4.5 ms | 26,257 / 401 |
| 100 | 357 ms | 3.4 ms | 62,617 / 617 |

Both give the same list (same `Min`). The calls are the `evaluate()` calls of
function expressions during the one call. Map's count grows as about 6N:
`Min(D)` in the body is evaluated once. Apply's count grows as about 6N²: the
body, and so `Min(D)` and the list `D` in it, is evaluated again for each
element. With `x` and `y` unassigned the ratio is the same (N = 64: 453 ms and
59,921 calls against 11 ms and 849). CE 0.192.0 gives the same counts, so this
is not a regression of the engine.

## Tycho impact

CE-1344 made `Apply(Function(body, k), d)` Tycho's one form for Desmos's
equal-bound sum `\sum_{i=d}^{d} B` (a zip broadcast over the list `d`), in
place of `Map`. In `neyret/lwuwgb9ic5` the reader `d_2 = Apply(k ↦ {k =
min(d): 10⁹, k}, d)` is evaluated by Tycho's row normalization
(`guardedEvaluate`). Paired rounds on 0.194.3, the Apply form (A) against the
same body as `Map` (B), resolve-and-compile time and allocation, medians of 9:

| State | A | B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| initial | 1,064 ms, 2.5 GB | 188 ms, 0.37 GB |
| N = 64 | 12,445 ms, 30.6 GB | 861 ms, 2.2 GB |
| N = 100 | 21,887 ms, 43.8 GB | 1,163 ms, 2.9 GB |

Tycho's reading of the CPU profiles at N = 64 (`prof-A/`, `prof-B/`; the
bundle is minified) puts 8,646 ms (A) against 670 ms (B) in the evaluate of the Apply's lazy collection (`_memoizedLazyCollectionValue`, an
element iterator that calls `evaluate()` per step, nested twice). Record:
Tycho `.cache/mg-paired-2026-11-20/` (`analysis-node.md`, `prof-A/`,
`prof-B/`, `B-map-scalar-split.patch`).

This is the slowdown that Tycho attached to CE-1563 ("A second effect: the
decline is slow"): it comes from the Apply form, not from the derivative
search, which that reading puts at about 1.3 s of the 13 s. The witness above
reproduces the per-element cost without Tycho.

## Cause and options

The evaluation of `Apply` of a function literal over a list substitutes each
element into the body and evaluates the whole body, where `Map` evaluates the
parts of the body that do not read the parameter once. Option (a,
recommended): evaluate `Apply(Function(…, k), L)` of a list `L` as `Map`
does, as CE-1344 describes the two forms as equal over a list. Option (b):
cache the value of a parameter-free subexpression of the body across the
elements. A possible workaround, writing `Map` when Tycho knows `d` is a list
(`B-map-scalar-split.patch`), brings back the collection oracle that CE-1344
removed; Tycho declined it (user decision 2026-11-20) and waits for this fix.

### The cause is the derivative of `Min` of a list, not `Apply`

CE (2026-11-20, session compute-engine-60): fixed by the commit that sets
this state. No change to `Apply`, to `Map` or to Tycho. To verify in Tycho:
`scripts/benchmark-macro-carriers.mts --doc neyret/lwuwgb9ic5@N=64` gives a
resolve-and-compile time under 1 s. The `applyVersusMap` reading of the
instrument does not change: the cost of each element of `Apply` is CE-1609.

What was observed. A CPU profile of the benchmark on the unchanged Tycho
c095ac5b7 at N = 64 puts 8.0 s of 12.4 s in one `evaluate()`, the left
side of row 49 in the fan-out probe (`guardedEvaluate`), and 3.8 s in six
evaluations of `D(m_2 - m, x)` and `D(m_2 - m, y)` during `resolveMember`,
0.65 s each (two come from Tycho's `evaluateDerivativeNode`,
`src/plot/ce-compile.ts:968`; the caller of the other four was not
recorded). Both
expressions carry their definitions, so they replay in CE alone. The
value of `D(m_2 - m, x)` has the type `vector<64>` for an expression of type
`number`: a `Which` of three lists, 3.1 million nodes. The value of the
quotient has 12.3 million nodes. Both consumers then discard the value (the
probe reads only whether it is a collection; the compile step declines "a
derivative with no closed form").

The cause. `D(Min([x^2, (x-1)^2]), x)` evaluated to the list
`[2x·Min′(…), (2x-2)·Min′(…)]`. The chain rule for an operator with no
derivative rule multiplies the partial derivative by the derivative of the
argument. For a list argument that the operator reduces to one number, the
partial derivative is a gradient and the product is a dot product, so the
element-wise product has the wrong shape. The Map form has the same defect
(`D(Min(Map(f, d)), x)` evaluates to a `Multiply` of type `vector<3>`); it
was only faster to compute. The cost came from the lists: each later
operator (`Power`, `Add`, `Sqrt`, `Divide`) was applied to 64 elements that
each hold the 64-term `Min`.

The fix. The chain rule declines when the call is a number and the argument,
or its derivative, is a collection, as it already does for a point argument.
`D(Min(list), x)` stays a `D`, and the quotient is a `Divide` of type
`number` with 50,000 nodes. Changed: `src/compute-engine/symbolic/derivative.ts`;
tests `test/compute-engine/ce-1602-derivative-of-list-reduction.test.ts`;
instrument in CE alone, with its output before and after,
`scripts/repros/2026-11-20-ce1602-derivative-of-list-reduction.mts`.

Measured on the unchanged Tycho c095ac5b7, CE ff12bbeb1 against the same
commit with the fix, production bundles, two alternating fresh-process
pairs under the box lock (load 3.5 on 18 cores). Resolve and compile, and V8
allocation, both rounds:

| List size | Control | Fix | Allocation, control / fix |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20 (as authored) | 1.17 s, 1.16 s | 0.24 s, 0.25 s | 3.4 GB / 0.7 GB |
| 64 | 10.61 s, 10.67 s | 0.55 s, 0.53 s | 30.3 GB / 1.5 GB |
| 100 | 21.26 s, 20.67 s | 0.94 s, 0.94 s | 47.1 GB / 2.8 GB |

The numeric digests and the generated source (11,358 and 12,830 characters,
JavaScript 2 of 2) are the same in every arm. The fix is faster than the
CE-only Map diagnostic on the same host (1.72 s and 2.94 s).

Snapshot refresh. On the unchanged Tycho, the control has a snapshot refresh
only at N = 100, and none at N = 20 or N = 64. The reason is in
`orchestrateRelationFamilyBaked` (`src/graph-paper/graph/relation-family.ts`):
when the probe answers "not a family", the row is baked
(`bakeResolvedRefs`), and a baked kernel has no refresh; at N = 100 the
probe exceeds its 10 s span, the fan returns a typed refusal, and the row
takes the ordinary route with live inputs. So every change of CE that
brings the probe under 10 s at N = 100 moves that size to the route that
N = 64 takes today, and the refresh at N = 100 cannot be kept by CE. With
the fix, N = 100 has no refresh, as N = 20 and N = 64. Inferred, not
measured in a browser: a change of N then costs one resolve (0.94 s)
where the control's refresh took 5.9 s. Keeping a refresh on the baked
route is a change in Tycho (publish the live inputs when the relation is
not a family); it is not needed for this fix.

What remains at N = 100: in a CPU profile of the fix, 0.83 s of the
probe's 0.96 s is the eager `Apply`, which evaluates `Equal(k, Min(d))`
once per cell, about 8 ms per cell with a 100-term symbolic `Min`. This is
CE-1609. Other wrong shapes of the same chain rule, with crafted witnesses,
are CE-1608.

A closed form was tried and is not part of the fix. The rule
`d/dx Min(L) = At(L′, ArgMin(L))` (and `ArgMax` for `Max`) gives the right
values (largest difference with a central difference over 30 points:
4·10⁻¹⁰), but on this document it is worse than the decline. One
fresh-process run on the unchanged Tycho c095ac5b7 (load 4.2 on 18 cores),
resolve and compile: 0.95 s, 6.59 s and 9.83 s at N = 20, 64 and 100,
against 0.25 s, 0.53 s and 0.94 s for the decline. The row has no
JavaScript kernel any more (JavaScript 1 of 2, no scalar sample). Three
reasons. Observed in CE alone: the value of `D(m_2 - m, x)` is 94,000
nodes at N = 64 and takes 1.1 s, because the evaluation writes out the
list of `Apply` with the 64-term `Min` in each element; and the compiler
does not support `ArgMin` on any target ("the operator ArgMin is not
supported by the compiler"). Observed in the benchmark log and read in
`compilation/ir/lower.ts` (`closedForm()`): a closed form of more than
10,000 nodes declines the compilation on JavaScript too ("a derivative
with no closed form of at most 10000 nodes"), where a `D` with no closed
form compiles to a numeric derivative. The patch
is `ce1602-fix-plus-closed-form.patch` in the evidence directory.
