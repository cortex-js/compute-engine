A convergent sum from 0 to infinity whose first term is 0, such as the sum of n/2^n, stays unevaluated at machine precision, while the same sum from 1 gives 2.

## Witness

At commit `e3c5a4248cf2d30a01f5d5c830e44f79b8537609`, `ce.precision = 'machine'`:

| `.N()` of | Gives | Must give |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Sum(n·2^−n, Limits(n, 0, +∞))` | unevaluated | 2 |
| `Sum(n·2^−n, Limits(n, 1, +∞))` | 1.9999999999999998 | 2 |
| `Sum(n·2^−\|n\|, Limits(n, 0, +∞))`, default precision | unevaluated | 2 |

Workaround: start the sum at 1.

## Cause and options

In `extrapolatedInfiniteSum()` (`library/utils.ts`), the first
extrapolation of the partial sums accepts 1.9999999999999998 with error 0.
The parity check, an extrapolation of the partial sums one step later
(`step: 2`), stops at once on `breaktol` with an error of 0.0156 and the
value 2.17, and the function then returns `undefined`: it does not try the
second acceptance (settled doubling windows), which accepts the sum from 1.
At default precision the compiled term `0` marks the compiled walk
unreliable, and the interpreter walk accepts the sum, except when the body
is not compiled (`Abs` is a jump function).

- When the parity check fails because its extrapolation stops early, fall
  through to the second acceptance instead of returning. The second
  acceptance keeps the check of the other parity (`|s3 − s0|`), so
  `Σ (−1)^k` still declines. Cost: a series that fails the parity check
  walks 2¹⁵ terms before it declines. Size S.

A sum over ℤ does not meet the case: its walk adds the pair
`f(j) + f(−j)` at each step, and `Σ n·2^−|n|` over ℤ gives 0.
