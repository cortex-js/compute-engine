A definite integral of a polynomial of degree above 8 times `e^x`, `sin x` or `cos x`, with a bound other than 0 or ±∞, stays unevaluated, and its exact closed form cannot be used.

## Witness

At `beefedb32d10044d76f6ca8de30379e7e52fccae` and with the change of CE-1249:

```ts
const ce = new ComputeEngine();
const I = (n, y) => ce.box(['Integrate',
  ['Multiply', ['Power', 'x', n], ['Exp', ['Negate', 'x']]],
  ['Limits', 'x', 0, y]]);
I(100, 20).evaluate().operator;   // 'Integrate' (the closed form exists)
I(100, 20).N();                   // 6.4321579720555e120 (quadrature, correct)
```

## Cause and options

The guard of CE-1311 in `library/calculus.ts` (the arm that reads
`unstableClosedForms`) keeps such an integral inert, so `.N()` takes the
quadrature. The decision of CE-1249 said to remove it, on the premise that
`.N()` of the closed form is correct once a cancelling sum is computed again
at a raised precision. The premise does not hold for every bound. With the
guard removed, `.evaluate().N()` gives:

- `∫₀^{1/10} x¹⁰⁰·e⁻ˣ dx`: `-2e+137` for `8.9675779765401e-104` (the two
  terms are about `9.3·10¹⁵⁷`, a loss of about 261 digits).
- `∫₀^{10⁻⁶} x¹⁰·e⁻ˣ dx`: `2e-14` for about `9.09e-68`.
- `∫₀^{10⁻¹²} x¹⁰·e⁻ˣ dx`: `0` for about `9.09e-134`.

The cause is the rule (2) of the CE-1249 decision: the first raise is at
most `2·working + 10` digits (52 at the default 21), and when the sum still
cancels there, it is not raised again and the working-precision value is
returned. So a sum that loses more than about `2·working + 7` digits (about
49 at 21 digits) keeps a value with no correct digit, whatever the limit of
200 extra digits.

`∫₀²⁰ x¹⁰⁰·e⁻ˣ dx` itself gives the correct `6.43215797205551260137e+120`.
The CE-1249 change kept the guard, so none of these wrong values ships.

Options:

- (a) Keep the guard (recommended). No wrong value; the integral stays
  unevaluated and `.N()` gives the quadrature value. Cost: none.
- (b) Remove the guard, and keep the closed form only when `.N()` of it is
  resolved within the raise limit: for example, compute the closed form at the
  bounds at a raised precision when the integral is evaluated, and keep the
  integral inert when the value keeps fewer than the working digits. Cost: a
  numeric evaluation at each `evaluate()` of such an integral, and one more
  rule in `library/calculus.ts`.
- (c) Remove the guard, and make `.N()` of a sum that does not resolve within
  the limit give no value (`NaN` or the sum unevaluated) rather than the
  working-precision result, or raise the precision again up to the limit
  (the original loop of `dc584a240b0a99e6e0a7e6e67c582973586bfcc1`, which
  rule (2) removed) and give no value past it. Cost: a change of the
  documented behavior of `.N()` for every exact sum that cancels by more
  than about `2·working + 7` digits, and for the second form the cost of up
  to 221 digits for an identity whose value is 0.
