A numeric integral with a power singularity close to 1/x at an endpoint other than 0 gives +∞ or NaN, although it converges.

## Witness

```
\int_0^1 (1-x)^{-0.99993}\,dx       .N() gives +∞, exact 14285.714…
\int_{0.25}^{1} (x-0.25)^{-0.9999}\,dx  .N() gives +∞, exact 9999.71…
\int_0^1 (1-x)^{-0.9993}\,dx        .N() gives NaN, exact 1428.57…
```

Readings of 2026-11-07 at `32c3c4337fe642b67aa1bc5ced671c84a7d84b47`, before
and after the CE-966 change. The same exponents at the endpoint 0
(`\int_0^1 x^{-0.99993}\,dx`) give the correct value.

## Cause and options

Not traced. Probable cause: at an endpoint `e ≠ 0`, the shells next to `e`
stop at `ACCURATE_SHELL_SPACINGS` spacings of the doubles at `e`
(`accurateShells()`, `numerics/endpoint-quadrature.ts`), so there are far
fewer shells than the 1019 at 0. For a shell ratio close to 1, they cannot
show convergence. NaN is within the error model; +∞, a divergence verdict for
a convergent integral, is the defect. Options: (a) give NaN, not +∞, when the
shells cannot tell convergence from divergence (simple and sound); (b) model
a geometric decrease of the shells in `shellTail()`.
