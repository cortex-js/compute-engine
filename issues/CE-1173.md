A thin space or another visual space directly after a minus sign or another prefix operator gives an error: `x = -\,b` is `x == (-Error(…), b)`.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-08, on `18c3c5a61d45e7d68140109181dd182702ffda90` with the
CE-1172 fix applied (the fix does not change these readings), with
`ce.parse(latex, { canonical: false }).json` and `ce.parse(latex).toString()`:

```
-\,b              InvisibleOperator(Negate(HorizontalSpacing(3)), b)                (-Error(ErrorCode("incompatible-type", "number", "nothing"), "Nothing"), b)
x=-\,b            Equal(x, InvisibleOperator(Negate(HorizontalSpacing(3)), b))      x == (-Error(…), b)
-\,\frac12        InvisibleOperator(Negate(HorizontalSpacing(3)), Divide(1, 2))     (-Error(…), 1/2)
\neg\,p           InvisibleOperator(Not(HorizontalSpacing(3)), p)                   (!Error(…), p)
\phantom{x}\,-b   Subtract(HorizontalSpacing(3), b)                                 -b + Error(…)   (a crafted input)
-\,1              Negate(1)                                                         -1      (correct: the number reader skips the space)
a-\,b             Subtract(a, InvisibleOperator(HorizontalSpacing(3), b))           a - b   (correct)
-b                Negate(b)                                                         -b      (control)
```

The workaround is to remove the spacing command.

## Cause and options

A prefix operator parses its operand above the precedence of a juxtaposition.
The primary parser reads the spacing command as a `HorizontalSpacing`
operand, and the operand ends there: the spacing command alone is the operand
of `Negate` or `Not`, and the symbol after it is juxtaposed to the result.
After an infix operator (`a-\,b`), the right operand is parsed at a lower
precedence, so the spacing command and the symbol are one juxtaposition, and
the canonical form of `InvisibleOperator` drops the spacing command.

In `\phantom{x}\,-b` the decoration is read first, so the rule of CE-1172
(`parsePrefixOperatorAfterSpacing()` in `latex-syntax/parse.ts`, visual space
and then a prefix operator at the start of an expression) does not apply.

Options:

- (a) When a prefix operator parses its operand, skip visual space first. The
  raw form of `-\,b` loses the spacing command, as the raw form of `\,-b`
  does. Size S. Recommended. `\phantom{x}\,-b` stays an error.
- (b) Skip visual space at the start of every expression that has no operand
  before it. This covers every reading above, and the raw form of `\,b` and
  of `a-\,b` loses the spacing command; `test/compute-engine/latex-syntax/structural-spacing.test.ts`
  reads raw forms that keep it.
