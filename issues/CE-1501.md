A page that shows a recursive function whose body takes the length of a parameter becomes slower each time it reads the function, until it stops responding.

## Witness

```ts
ce.declare('P', { signature: '(unknown, unknown) -> unknown' });
ce.assign('P', ce.box(['Function',
  ['Which', ['Equal', 'n', 0], 'x', 'True',
    ['Add', ['Length', 'n'], ['P', ['Subtract', 'n', 1], 'x']]], 'n', 'x']));
for (let i = 0; i < 4; i++) String(ce.box(['P', 1, 2]).type);
```

Each boxing of `P(1, 2)` gives a deeper type,
`broadcastable<broadcastable<…>>`: the type text has 93, 168, then 243
characters (0.193.0 and 4779733). With `P(n, x)` in place of `P(n - 1, x)`
(the Tycho test), the same, and a fourth boxing gives 318 characters; the four
boxings took about 10 ms, 30 ms, 0.5 s and 12 to 16 s (two runs on each
version). With `n` in place of `Length(n)`, the type is `integer` at every
boxing.

Not a regression: 0.193.0 does the same. Tycho met it at the adoption: with a
Tycho change (since reverted) the test did not end, as the implementer
reported.

## Expected

The same expression gets the same type at each boxing, and the inference of a
recursive call ends at a fixed point or a bound (for example `unknown`).

## Instrument

`npx tsx scripts/repros/2026-11-18-ce1497-ce1498-ce1499-ce1500-ce1501-compile-gaps-probe.mts` in Tycho. The case runs in a child process with a limit of 30 s. `CE-1501: READY`
when three boxings give type texts of one length. NOT READY on 0.193.0 and
on 4779733.
