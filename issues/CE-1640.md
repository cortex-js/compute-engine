The derivative of an expanded or simplified expression, written as one expression, is returned without being computed.

## Witness

MathJSON, evaluated at `edccc20202dd5567a15031af3094240a7c52c04f`:

```
["D", ["Expand", ["Power", ["Add", "x", 1], 2]], "x"]
    → ["D", ["Expand", ["Power", ["Add", "x", 1], 2]], "x"]     (expected 2x + 2)
["D", ["Simplify", ["Power", ["Add", "x", 1], 2]], "x"]
    → ["D", ["Simplify", ["Power", ["Add", "x", 1], 2]], "x"]   (expected 2x + 2)
```

Controls: `["D", ["Power", ["Add", "x", 1], 2], "x"]` is `2x + 2`;
`["Integrate", ["Expand", …], "x"]` is `x³/3 + x² + x`; and
`["Limit", ["Expand", …], "x", 1]` is `4`. The result is not a wrong value:
the derivative stays unevaluated.

Workaround: apply the transformer to the derivative (`Expand(D(e, x))`), or
store the expanded expression first and differentiate the stored value.

## Cause and options

`Solve`, `Integrate` and `Limit` reduce an operand whose head is a transformer
(`Expand`, `Simplify`, `Factor`, `Together`, …) with `reduceTransformerHead()`
(`src/compute-engine/boxed-expression/utils.ts`, `TRANSFORMER_HEADS`) before
their algorithm reads it. The evaluation of `D`
(`src/compute-engine/symbolic/derivative.ts`) does not, and it has no
derivative rule for these heads.

Option: `D` reduces the transformer heads of its first operand as `Limit` does
(`symbolic/limit.ts`: `withValueShield(ce, [x], () => reduceTransformerHead(b))`),
with the variable of differentiation shielded. Size S.
