A convergent integral from a large number to infinity gives `NaN` when it is computed numerically.

## Witness

```
\int_{10^{10}}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{1.5}}  .N()   gives NaN, exact 0.00002
\int_{10^{6}}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x(\ln x)^2} .N()  gives NaN, exact 0.0723824
```

Readings of 2026-11-07, before and after the CE-182 change.

## Cause and options

`quadrature` (`numerics/gauss-kronrod.ts`) maps `[c, ∞)` to `t ∈ [0, 1)`.
For a large `c`, the area is where 1 − t < 1/c, next to t = 1, where the
doubles are 2⁻⁵³ apart, and the divergence test reports a divergence. The
computation in two parts with the infinite bound at 0 (CE-182) runs only for
a `singularCorner` result; its parts converge to the exact values here.

Option (a): also compute a `divergent` result in two parts, and keep them
only when they converged or were extrapolated and `smoothTail` accepts the
far part. Cost: two more quadratures for each divergent integral over an
infinite bound. Option (b): scale the change of variable with `c`.
