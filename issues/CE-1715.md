At the default precision, the numeric value of √2 times a list of fractions has a wrong last digit and more digits than the precision: `N(√2·[1/3])` gives `0.471404520791031682933424928812542301650399437`, where `N(√2·(1/3))` gives `0.4714045207910316829338962`.

## Witness

At `fd60d3b6e448b38133e4910a9cb923cd53d740e9` (and with the CE-1710 and
CE-1711 change in the working tree), default precision (21 digits):

```js
ce.box(['Multiply', ['Sqrt', 2], ['List', ['Rational', 1, 3], 0.5]]).N().json;
// ["List", {num: "0.471404520791031682933424928812542301650399437"},
//          {num: "0.7071067811865475244008445"}]
ce.box(['Multiply', ['Sqrt', 2], ['Rational', 1, 3]]).N().json;
// {num: "0.4714045207910316829338962"}
```

At 21 digits the cell reads `…682933` and the scalar product `…682934`. The
float cell `0.5` gives the right value; the fraction cell does not. At machine
precision both agree (`0.4714045207910317`).

The same family, from the review of the CE-1710/1711 change: a list of
floats that holds its numbers unboxed goes to the big-decimal kernel
(`bigFloatBroadcast()`), whose cells have the working precision, where the
scalar product carries more digits:

```js
ce.box(['Multiply', ['Rational', 1, 3], ['List', 0.5, 0.25]]).N().json[1];
// {num: "0.166666666666666666667"}
ce.box(['Multiply', ['Rational', 1, 3], 0.5]).N().json;
// {num: "0.16666666666666666666666667"}
```

At 21 digits these two agree; the JSON differs. The cells of a product with
a list then depend on how the list is stored, and a test that compares them
with the scalar products at this precision fails.

## Cause and options

Not confirmed. Beside an exact radical factor, `mulTensors()`
(`boxed-expression/arithmetic-mul-div.ts`) rounds each cell of the list first
(`nKeepingExactCells()` declines there, since the scalar product folds
`√2·(1/3)` into the exact `√2/3` before it rounds). The cell product then
multiplies the big-decimal `√2` by the rounded `1/3` and keeps every digit of
the product, unrounded to the working precision. A fix rounds the cell
product to the precision, or folds an exact radical factor with an exact
cell as the scalar product does.
