A solver whose equation takes the mean or the median of a list that reads the unknown no longer finds the solution.

## Witness

```
FindRoot([Equal(6, Mean([a, 2a]))], [(a, 1)])
```

0.196.0: `a = 1`, `converged: false`, `residualNorm: 4.5`, 1 iteration.
0.195.1: `a = 4`, `converged: true`, 3 iterations. The same with `Median` in
place of `Mean`. Control: `Max([a, 2a]) = 8` converges to `a = 4` on both.
`compile()` of `6 - Mean([a, 2a])` and `N()` at a given `a` give the right
values on both releases.

## Cause and options

On 0.196.0, `D(Mean([a, 2a]), a)` stays unevaluated as `D(Mean(…), a)`; on
0.195.1 it was a list of `Derivative(Mean)` terms. This probably comes with
the change of CE-1602 (the derivative of a number computed from a list).
`FindRoot` appears to take the unevaluated derivative as its Jacobian and stop.
Options: (a) give `Mean` and `Median` of a list the derivative that the
CHANGELOG gives `Min` and `Max` (`Mean`: the mean of the derivatives); (b) in
`FindRoot`, use a numeric Jacobian when the symbolic derivative does not
evaluate to a number. Both are wanted: (b) protects every other reduction.

## CE answer

CE (2026-11-21): P1 confirmed. On main at `672b22cfa`, `Mean` and
`Median` reproduce the failure while `Max` converges. The fitting code
already chooses finite differences for invalid derivatives and unresolved
`Derivative` expressions. CE-1602 can leave an unresolved `D`, which had
escaped that check. Adding `D` to the existing check makes both reported
cases converge to `a = 3.999999999889048` in three iterations. The `Max`
control is unchanged. The patch matches the `D` operator and keeps numeric
probes within declared bounds, with an interior retry at a singular
endpoint. The resulting patch passes all 26 targeted FindRoot tests and
typecheck. Until a release
contains the fix, the reported two-element `Mean` and `Median` can both
be written as the sum of their elements divided by two.
