A convergent integral of 1/(t·(−ln t)^p) with p slightly above 1 gives +∞.

## Witness

```
\int_0^{1/2} \frac{dt}{t(-\ln t)^{1.001}}   .N() gives +∞, exact 1000.3666
\int_0^{1/2} \frac{dt}{t(-\ln t)^{1.005}}   .N() gives +∞, exact 200.3668
\int_0^{1/2} \frac{dt}{t(-\ln t)^{1.01}}    .N() gives 100.364 ± 0.010 (correct)
```

Readings of 2026-11-07. The integral converges for every p > 1, a textbook
exercise. The same integrand with its singularity at the bound 1
(`(1 − t)(−ln(1 − t))^p` on `[1/2, 1]`) gives a wide error bar instead.

## Cause and options

Not traced. The probable cause: the divergence test of the adaptive
quadrature (`shellsDiverge`, `numerics/gauss-kronrod.ts`) reads shells that
decrease like `1/k^p`, with `p` near 1, as shells that do not decrease.
Option: when the shells decrease
like a power of `k` with an exponent above 1, do not report a divergence;
give the result with the error of the shell model, or a wide error.
