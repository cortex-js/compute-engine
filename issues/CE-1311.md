The numeric value of the exact definite integral of x^100 times e^(-x) from 0 to 20 is 0, where the true value is about 6.43e120.

## Witness

At `fd54cbe9b80182f1b3403b9c303ab2e1baeec90d`, `new ComputeEngine()` (precision 21):

```ts
const I = ce.box(['Integrate', ['Multiply', ['Power', 'x', 100], ['Exp', ['Negate', 'x']]], ['Limits', 'x', 0, 20]]);
I.evaluate().toString(); // 100! - 4.5278…e166 / e^20   (exact, correct)
I.evaluate().N().toString(); // 0                       (expected 6.4321579720555…e120)
I.N().toString();            // 6.4321579720555e+120 ± 9.1e+108 (correct, quadrature)
```

In 0.189.0, `evaluate()` left the integral unevaluated (the by-parts search
spent its budget), and `.N()` of it took the quadrature, which is right.

## Cause and options

`evaluate()` now gives the exact closed form of CE-1282, `n! − e⁻ʸ·Σ n!/k!·yᵏ`,
which is right. `.N()` of it evaluates the two terms, each about 9.3e157, at 21
digits, and their difference (about 37 digits smaller) cancels to 0. This is the
class of CE-1249 (`.N()` of an exact difference that cancels at the working
precision), with a plausible witness. The compiled route was fixed by CE-1310
(it no longer compiles this closed form above degree 8). Options: (a) `.N()`
of an `Add` whose result is much smaller than its largest term evaluates again
at a higher precision (fixes CE-1249 too); (b) `evaluate()` of a definite
integral whose integrand is a polynomial of degree above 8 times exp, sin or cos
of a linear argument keeps the integral unevaluated when a bound is finite
and not 0 (loses the exact value). (a) is recommended.
