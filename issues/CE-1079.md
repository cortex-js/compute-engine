The numeric value of the conjugate of a long list of complex numbers is the list itself, not its conjugate.

## Witness

At machine precision, with `B` a list of 101 complex floats:

```js
ce.precision = 'machine';
ce.assign('B', ce.box(['List', ...Array(101).fill(['Complex', 0.5, 1.25])]));
ce.box(['Conjugate', 'B']).N().at(1).toString();        // (0.5 + 1.25i): wrong
ce.box(['Conjugate', 'B']).evaluate().at(1).toString(); // (0.5 - 1.25i)
```

With 100 elements, `N()` gives `0.5 - 1.25i`. The same holds for
`Conjugate(A / B)` and `Conjugate(A · B)`. At the default precision the value
is correct.

## Cause and options

Not traced. Above 100 elements the broadcast is a lazy `Map`; under `N()`,
its element loses the `Conjugate` at machine precision only. Find the step in
`lazyBroadcastMap()` or the `N()` rewrap of the `Map`
(`collection-utils.ts`) that drops it.
