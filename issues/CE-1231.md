A compiled `Limit` at `-∞` with no direction gives the limit at `+∞`.

## Witness

At `edf0c42631a479230ffbb04bcd33d745725ceeed`, with the default options of
`compile()` and `a` declared `real`:

```js
const e = ce.box(['Limit', ['Function', ['Arctan', ['Multiply', 'a', 't']], 't'], 'NegativeInfinity']);
const f = compile(e);
f.code;           // _SYS.limit(((t) => Math.atan(_.a * t)), -Infinity)
f.run({ a: 2 });  // 1.5707963267948966
e.subs({ a: 2 }).N(); // -1.5707963267948966
```

A limit with no input is folded by the symbolic attempt with the default
options, and is right there (`Limit(t ↦ arctan t, -∞)` is
`(-1.5707963267948966)`); with `constantFold: false` it is
`_SYS.limit(((t) => Math.atan(t)), -Infinity)`, `1.5707963267948966`.
`Limit(t ↦ t/|t|, -∞)` gives `1` where the interpreter gives `-1` (the
random tester). A point that is infinite only at run time has the same
defect: with `ai: real | signed_infinity`, `Limit(t ↦ arctan t, ai)` gives
π/2 at `ai = -∞`.

The runtime routine reads an infinite point as the infinity of the sign of
the direction: `limit(atan, x, d)` (`numerics/numeric.ts`) gives `π/2` for
`(+∞, 1)` and `(-∞, 1)`, `-π/2` for `(+∞, -1)` and `(-∞, -1)`, and `NaN` for
a direction 0. The interpreter gives the limit at the infinity of the point
for every direction.

The `numeric-kernel` profile declines a literal point `±∞` whose sign is
not the sign of the direction, and a point whose type admits an infinity
(slice 4).

## Cause and options

`limit()` uses the direction, not the sign of `x`, for an infinite `x`.
Options: in `limit()`, take the sign of an infinite `x` as the direction
(the direction then means nothing at an infinity, as on the interpreter);
or in the `Limit` handler of `javascript-target.ts`, pass `-1` for a point
`-∞`. The first also covers a point that is infinite at run time.
