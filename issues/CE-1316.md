The largest or smallest value of a mapped list whose function holds a sum or
a product over its own index evaluates to an expression in that index, where
the numeric evaluation gives the number.

## Witness

At `be4c7f7097a68995314c3d38bddc87a30f567228`, `new ComputeEngine()`,
`ce.box(…)`:

| Program | `evaluate()` | `N()` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Max(Map(Function(Sum(k, Limits(k, 1, 3)), j), List(10, 20)))` | `Max(3k, 3k)` | `6` |
| `Max(Map(Function(Product(k, Limits(k, 1, 3)), j), List(10, 20)))` | `Max(k^3, k^3)` | `6` |
| `Min(Map(Function(Product(k, Limits(k, 1, 3)), j), List(10, 20)))` | `Min(k^3, k^3)` | `6` |
| `Max(Map(Function(Product(k, Limits(k, 1, 3)), k), List(10, 20)))` | `6` | `6` (control: the parameter is named `k`) |
| `Apply(Function(Product(k, Limits(k, 1, 3)), j), 10)` | `6` | `6` (control) |

The right value of the first three rows is `6`: the sum and the product bind
`k`, and `3k` and `k^3` read `k` as a free name, as if the body `k` were
summed or multiplied three times without its index.

## Cause and options

Not investigated. The difference between the rows is the name of the
parameter of the function: with a parameter that is not the index, the index
of the sum is read as a free name when `Max` or `Min` evaluates the mapped
list. `Map` alone stays unevaluated (lazy), so the path is the evaluation of
the lazy list by the reduction.
