The remainder of an exact tie rounds the wrong way when the dividend is a fraction and the divisor a decimal number: it gives +0.35 where the documented answer is −0.35.

## Witness

On main `846a378cb`, `new ComputeEngine()`: with `x` the exact rational of the double −0.35 and the divisor the exact decimal of the double 0.7 (so `x / 0.7` is exactly −1/2), `Remainder(x, 0.7)` gives `+0.35`. With both operands as decimals, or both as doubles, it gives `−0.35`, the documented tie toward +∞ (`round(−1/2) = 0`, so the remainder is `x` itself).

The same tie on `new ComputeEngine({ precision: 'machine' })`, `x` declared `real` and given the value `-0.35` with `ce.withValues`: `Remainder(x, 0.7)` evaluates to `+0.35`; the interval mode gives the documented `−0.35`. `test/compute-engine/ir-interval-containment.test.ts` excuses this point in `INTERPRETER_DEFECTS`; remove the excuse with the fix.

## Cause and options

Not investigated. Probably the rational/decimal path of `Remainder` in `library/arithmetic.ts` rounds the quotient with a rule other than the double path's `Math.round` (ties toward +∞). Fix: use one tie rule in every numeric path.
