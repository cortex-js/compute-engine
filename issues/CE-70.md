`Expand`, `Factor`, `Together` and similar operators give no error for an absent argument, while other operators refuse it.

## Witness

With `L = [1, 2]`, `Expand(At(L, 5))` and `Factor(At(L, 5))` give
`At([1, 2], 5)` (checked 2026-11-06). By decision P2 (a) of the plan below, an
absent argument is refused uniformly, with an error that names the parameter.

## Cause and options

Plan: `docs/plans/2026-10-21-presence-contracts-and-assertion.md` (decisions P1
to P5; §9 is the record). The operators that hold their operands (`lazy`:
`Expand`, `ExpandAll`, `Factor`, `Together`, `Distribute`, `Cancel`,
`PartialFraction`, `Apart`, `ToCNF`) are exempt from the refusal of slice S5
(`refusesAbsentOperandAt`). Fix: apply the refusal to them.

A `missing` element inside a value (`list<string | missing>`) is judged by the
element contract (decision P1b). The other parts of the old entry are CE-1000
and CE-1001.
