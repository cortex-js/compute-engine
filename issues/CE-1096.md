Above machine precision, a number plus an imaginary number keeps only about 16 digits of the imaginary part.

## Witness

At precision 30 (`npx tsx scripts/probe.ts --precision 30 '<latex>'`):

```
1+0.123456789012345678901234567i
canonical → ["Complex", 1, 0.12345678901234568]

0.123456789012345678901234567i
canonical → ["Complex", 0, {"num": "0.123456789012345678901234567"}]
```

The MathJSON `["Add", 1, ["Complex", 0, {"num": "0.123456789012345678901234567"}]]`
gives the same 17 digits. The imaginary number alone keeps all its digits,
and so does `["Multiply", 2, ["Complex", 1, {"num": "0.123456789012345678901234567"}]]`
(`["Complex", 2, {"num": "0.246913578024691357802469134"}]`), so the loss is
in the canonical sum of a real number and a complex number.

## Cause and options

Not traced. The probable cause: the canonical form of the sum builds the
complex result from the double parts of its operands (`re` and `im`), not from their big decimals
(`bignumRe` and `bignumIm` of `BigNumericValue`). Option: build the result
from the big decimals when an operand is a big-number float.
