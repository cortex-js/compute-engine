In the editor, the hover of a name in a function written above the `let` of that name shows the library function, and a rename of the `let` does not change the name in that function.

## Witness

With the fix of CE-1826, on a new engine:

```
let h = () => mean(3)
let mean = (x) => x + 4
h()
```

The program gives `7`: `mean` in the body of `h` is the variable.
`epsilHover(ce, source, 14)` gives `kind: "library-function"` for that
`mean`, and `epsilDefinition(ce, source, 14)` gives `null`. They must give
the variable and the `let` statement. With a name that the library does not
have (`m`), the hover gives no kind and the definition is `null` too. A
rename of the `let` (the occurrences of `documentBindings()`) does not
rewrite the name in the body of `h`, for any name.

## Cause and options

`epsilHover()` and `epsilDefinition()` (`src/epsil/hover.ts`), the
completions (`src/epsil/completion.ts`) and the static pass
(`src/epsil/static-diagnostics.ts`) read `documentBindings()`
(`src/epsil/occurrences.ts`) in its default model, where a `let` is visible
from the end of its statement. The interpreter, `resolveLibraryNames()` and
`epsilProgramSites()` use the `engineRules` option: a `let` binds its name in
its whole block.

Option (a): the editor callers pass `engineRules`. Under that option an
assignment never declares, so the pinned case "a first assignment declares"
of `test/compute-engine/epsil-hover.test.ts` (`a = 1`, `a = 2`, `a` gives the
definition `a = 1`) gives `null`, and the pinned occurrences of
`test/epsil/occurrences.test.ts` change. The interpreter reports
`assignment-to-undeclared-variable` for that program, so the new answer
agrees with it.
Option (b): a separate option of `documentBindings()` that only makes a
`let` visible in its whole block, and keeps the other rules of the editor
model.
