At the default precision, the numeric value of a fraction times the square root of a negative number has a wrong last digit and more digits than the precision: `N((1/3)·√-2)` gives `0.471404520791031682933424928812542301650399437i`, where `N(√-2/3)` gives `0.4714045207910316829338962i`.

## Witness

At `7dcd2d6b8cfe55be8d61bd25c79bb6fe802f0ec0`, default precision (21 digits):

```js
ce.box(['Multiply', ['Rational', 1, 3], ['Sqrt', -2]]).N().json;
// ["Complex", 0, {num: "0.471404520791031682933424928812542301650399437"}]
ce.box(['Divide', ['Sqrt', -2], 3]).N().json;
// ["Complex", 0, {num: "0.4714045207910316829338962"}]
```

At 21 digits the first reads `…682933` and the second `…682934`. The same
digits appear in each cell of `√-2·[1/3, 0.5]`.

## Cause and options

Not confirmed. The canonical form of `Divide(Sqrt(-2), 3)` is the exact
number `Complex(0, √2/3)`, but the canonical form of
`Multiply(1/3, Sqrt(-2))` keeps `Sqrt(-2)` as a function. Under `.N()` the
product then multiplies the big decimal of `√2` by the rounded `1/3` and keeps
every digit of the product of two big decimals. The CE-1715 fold in
`mulNEvaluated()` (`arithmetic-mul-div.ts`) does not fire, because
`Sqrt(-2)` is not an exact number literal. A fix makes the canonical product
fold `Sqrt(-2)` with an exact rational, as `Divide` does, or rounds a product
of big decimals to the working precision.
