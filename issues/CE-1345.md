Building an expression that reuses a list through `Zip` many times takes a time that doubles at each level of reuse, so a deep one never finishes.

## Witness

```ts
const ce = new ComputeEngine();
ce.declare('x', 'real');
ce.declare('A', 'list<real>');
let z = ce.box('A');
for (let i = 0; i < L; i++)
  z = ce.function('Zip', [z, ce.function('Multiply', [ce.box('x'), z])]);
```

The canonical construction of the levels takes 176 ms at `L = 8`, 355 ms at
10, 999 ms at 12 (each level reads `z` twice: a shared graph of `2^L` paths);
with `Map(Function(At(Z, 1) + At(Z, 2), Z), Zip(…))` around each level, 1.9 s
at 12 and about 7.7 s at 14. The same construction with `ListJoin`, `List` or
`Add` in place of `Zip` takes 1 to 10 ms at 12 levels. Measured with
`npx tsx` on the tree of 77a218dc44791baf207ad7733817e2d498e98939.

## Cause and options

Not investigated: the canonical form or the type of `Zip` reads its operands
once per path (no memo per node). The fix is to find the walk (`Zip` in
`library/collections.ts`, its type or canonical handler) and read each operand
once. Size S once found.
