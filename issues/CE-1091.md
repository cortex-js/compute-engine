The type of a call of one of two functions that call each other does not hold its value when the other one was typed first.

## Witness

```
f, g declared (unknown) -> unknown, m: integer
f := n ↦ If(n = 0, "a", g(n − 1))
g := n ↦ If(n = 0, 2, f(n − 1))
type of f(m) read first, then:
g(m)                         type number (checked 2026-11-07)
g(1)                         value "a"
```

The type of `g(m)` must hold `"a"` (`integer | string`, or `unknown`).
Checked in the worktree of CE-836 (base
`8651a23b7c9f7b38d95ba3f67caac940b4128c9f` with the CE-836 change); the
reviewer of CE-836 reports the same result at that base without the change.

## Cause and options

Not traced. The call `g(n − 1)` in the body of `f` is typed while the result
of `f` is derived, and `f(n − 1)` in the body of `g` is then a recursive call
that is left out of the join. A type of `g` computed in that window seems to
be kept after it, under the declared signatures. The CE-836 change (a type
that leaves a recursive call out is not kept by the type cache,
`unknownCallMayBeRecursive()` in `declared-parameter-result.ts`) does not
reach this case: the call may be typed by the declared-signature derivation
(`_deriveSignature()`), which keeps a memo of its own.
