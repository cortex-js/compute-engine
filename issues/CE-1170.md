A thin space or another visual space before a plus or a minus sign makes the sign a multiplication: `a\, + 1` gives `a`, and `a\, - 1` gives `-a`.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-08, on `c726cde71a07c5fb18835db9fa933c68a2d52327`, with
`ce.parse(latex, { canonical: false }).json` and `ce.parse(latex).toString()`:

```
a\, + 1             InvisibleOperator(a, 1)                    a
a\, - 1             InvisibleOperator(a, -1)                   -a
2\, - 1                                                        1
\int x\,dx\, + 1    InvisibleOperator(Integrate(x, x), 1)
a + 1               Add(a, 1)                                  a + 1   (correct)
```

LaTeX writers put `\,` before a sign to adjust the spacing, for example after
an integrand.

## Cause and options

Not traced. The raw parse has no `HorizontalSpacing` operand: the infix loop
of `parseExpression()` (`latex-syntax/parse.ts`) skips the visual space, but
the `+` or `-` after it is then read as a prefix sign of a juxtaposed operand,
not as the infix operator. `skipDecorationBeforeInfix()` and the
`InvisibleOperator` route of the same loop are the first places to read.

The CE-1160 fix moves the index past such a space when the body of an
integral stops at the sign after its integrand, so
`\int \frac{dx}{x}\, - 1` is correct; the general case is not.
