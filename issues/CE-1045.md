An integral whose fraction with the differential comes before another factor, `\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x}\cdot 2`, gives a wrong value.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-07, at commit `2b079836c9e2906a1bc92263018a1ea4d2cdb703`:

```
\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x}\cdot 2   parses to Integrate(Multiply(Divide(d·x, x), 2), Limits(Nothing, 1, 2))
                               evaluate() gives 3; the value is 2 ln 2 ≈ 1.386
\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x} 2        the same, with InvisibleOperator: evaluate() gives 3
\int_1^2 2\frac{dx}{x}         gives 2 ln 2 (correct after CE-1030)
\int_0^1 x(dx)                 the integration variable is d; evaluate() gives x^2/2
```

In `x(dx)`, CE-1030 keeps the parentheses on purpose: `f(dx)` can be a call.

## Cause and options

`parseSubintegrand()` in `latex-syntax/dictionary/definitions-calculus.ts`
searches a product for the differential only at its end and in its last
factor. When the fraction that holds the differential is followed by another
factor, no differential is found, and the canonical form takes the free
symbol `d` as the integration variable.

Options: (a) search every factor, and take the differential out only when
exactly one factor holds one; (b) replace a factor that holds a differential
not at the end by an `unexpected-differential` error, as
`markDenominatorDifferentials()` does. (b) is simpler and gives no wrong value.
