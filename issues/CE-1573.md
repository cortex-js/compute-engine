`LogGamma` of a long exact rational next to a zero of ln|Γ(x)| at x < 0 is wrong from about the 19th digit at 30 digits.

## Witness

`LogGamma(-245702473822080062302945414765117954323659791 / 10^44)`, near the
zero of ln|Γ| at x ≈ −2.457:

| precision | real part |
| --- | --- |
| 30 | `1.51560344802165732162470016875e-20` |
| 80 | `1.5156034480216573216856410677875751901787707521017…e-20` |

The 30-digit value is wrong from its 19th digit. A decimal operand with the
same digits is right: `BigNumericValue.N()` keeps all of its input digits.

## Cause and options

`guardDigitsN` (`library/exact-operands-under-n.ts`) reads an exact operand at
the working precision plus 10 digits. Near a zero of ln|Γ| the result is many
orders below the operand's rounding error, and the retry loop of the LogGamma
kernel (`numerics/special-functions.ts`) raises the arithmetic precision but
reuses the rounded operand. Options: the kernel re-reads the exact operand at
each retry (it needs the exact value passed down), or it declines when the
measured cancellation is larger than the operand's guard.
