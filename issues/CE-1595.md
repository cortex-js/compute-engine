A heatmap that uses gcd or lcm of slider-based values draws on the CPU, not the GPU, so it is slow to redraw.

## Witness

The showcase prototype colours the multiplication table mod n with
`gcd(mod(⌊nx⌋⌊ny⌋, n), n)`, where `x`, `y` and the slider `n` are `real`.
On 0.194.3 the GLSL target declines it: "GCD (the operation rgcd) on glsl".
`GCD(x, y)` and `LCM(x, y)` with real `x`, `y` decline the same way (`rlcm`).
Controls: `GCD(floor(x), floor(y))` and `LCM(floor(x), floor(y))` compile to
GLSL (`_gpu_ir_gcd`), and `GCD(x, y)` with real operands compiles to
JavaScript and gives 6 at (12, 18).

## Cause and options

The GLSL target has the integer kernel but not the real-operand one that the
JavaScript target uses (there, `GCD(12.5, 18)` gives 0.5). Option: a GLSL
`rgcd`/`rlcm` with the semantics of the JavaScript kernel; for integer
values it is the integer loop. Size S.

## CE answer

CE (2026-11-20): P2 confirmed, fixed in the commit after `fixed-in`. `GCD`
and `LCM` of real operands compile to GLSL (`_gpu_ir_rgcd`, `_gpu_ir_rlcm`),
as before 0.194.0. The witness and `GCD(x, y)`, `LCM(x, y)` give the
interpreter's value in WebGL 2 where the operands hold integers (exact,
below 2^31) and where they have a common divisor far above 1e-6 of the
larger operand (`GCD(12.5, 18)` is 0.5). Two limits are in the
known-limitations list of `docs/COMPILATION-MODEL.md`: integers of 2^31 or
more give NaN, and operands with no common divisor above 1e-6 of the larger
one give another small value than the interpreter's, because the remainders
are single floats. `interval-js` still declines. No workaround is necessary;
before the fix, `GCD(floor(x), floor(y))` compiled.
