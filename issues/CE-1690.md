A small number printed in engineering notation shows a value 10,000 times too large.

## Witness

```js
ce.number(0.000012).toLatex({ notation: 'engineering', avoidExponentsInRange: null });
// ➔ "120.0\cdot10^{-3}"   (that is 0.12; it must be 12.0\cdot10^{-6})
```

`0.0012` gives `1.2\cdot10^{-3}` and `0.12` gives `120.0\cdot10^{-3}`, both
right: every exponent from -4 down that is not a multiple of 3 is wrong.
`parse()` of the output gives `0.12`. The same function writes Epsil numbers
(`serializeEpsil`), which therefore does not expose `notation` in its
`numberFormat` option.

## Cause and options

`serializeScientificNotationNumber` (`latex-syntax/serialize-number.ts`) moves
the exponent to a multiple of 3 with
`adjust = -((expMultiple + exponent) % expMultiple)` for a negative exponent,
then adds `adjust` to the exponent and moves the decimal point `|adjust|`
places to the right. For an exponent of -5 that gives -3 and `120.`, where it
must give -6 and `12.`: the exponent must go down by
`((exponent % 3) + 3) % 3` and the point move right by the same count, for
both signs. Size S, with a test of exponents -1 to -8 and 1 to 8 read back by
`parse()`.
