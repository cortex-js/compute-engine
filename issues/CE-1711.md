The numeric value of a number times a list that mixes fractions with other cells has a wrong last digit: `N(3·[1/3, 0.5])` gives `0.999…9` for the first cell at 21 digits, where `N(3·(1/3))` gives `1`.

## Witness

At `bc7d8f7875aa0d1450f297269b85b8c08689c5e3`, with the default precision:

```js
ce.box(['Multiply', 3, ['List', ['Rational', 1, 3], 0.5]]).N().json;
// ["List", {num: "0.999999999999999999999"}, 1.5]
```

The same with a symbol, `NaN` or `Missing` cell beside the fraction
(`3·[1/3, x]`), with a complex cell (`(1/3)·[3 + 6i]`), and with a tuple of
fractions (`3·(1/3, 2/3)` gives `(0.999…9, 2.000…1)`). CE-1653 keeps a list
exact only when every cell is an exact integer or fraction and every factor
is exact; a mixed list is rounded cell by cell first, as before.

## Cause and options

`mulTensors()` and the `Multiply` handler
(`isExactRationalListLiteral()`, `boxed-expression/arithmetic-mul-div.ts`;
`mulEvaluatedOperands()`, `library/arithmetic.ts`) round every cell of a list
that is not all exact before the cell products. The fix: keep the exact cells
of any list literal exact and round the other cells, so that each cell
product folds an exact cell with the exact factors; and give a `Tuple`
literal of exact cells the rule of a `List` literal.
