A compiled function that reads one part of a tuple whose parts have different kinds falls back to the interpreter in some forms.

## Witness

From the decline census on GitHub #638: 10 family operations and maps of a
combinatorics corpus (two census groups). On main f42a02d1323b9e40cf2def511715fc3034ae8d6c, JavaScript target:

```ts
ce.declare('S', 'tuple<string, integer>');
ce.declare('P', 'list<integer>');
compile(ce.box(['Last', 'S']), { fallback: false });
// Could not compile: S: S, a tuple whose parts have different lanes, other
// than in a read of a part at a constant index
compile(ce.box(['At', ['Tuple', 0, 'P'], 1]), { fallback: false });
// Could not compile: Tuple(0, P): elements of two types
```

`At(T, 1)` with `T: tuple<list<integer>, list<integer>>` compiles. `fallback: true` gives the interpreter's value (the workaround).

## Cause and options

`Last`, `First` and `At` at a constant position read one part, whose shape is
settled at compile time, as `At(T, 1)` already does: lower `First` and `Last`
of a tuple to that read. A tuple literal read at a constant position can be
folded to that part before its other parts are lowered. `At(T, k)` at a
run-time `k` of such a tuple stays declined: its shape depends on `k`.
