Whether a very deeply nested expression compiles with the new compiler
depends on the size of the call stack of the program that runs the engine.

## Witness

At `511a446b8c4dc43b50c9fac75c6735861b6be580` plus the colour batch, with
`compile(expr, { profile: 'ir', fallback: false })`, on both targets: a chain
of 1,000 nested `AsOklch` around a colour (`AsOklch(AsOklch(… Oklch(x, 0.1,
30) …))`) compiles under Jest and declines with "an expression too deep to
compile" under `tsx`, where the call stack is smaller. A chain of 200 compiles
under both. `Sin` nested 20,000 deep declines under both.

Related, in the engine and before any compilation: a chain of 1,000 nested
`If` (`If(Greater(x, i), Sin(x), e)`) makes `.type` throw "Maximum call stack
size exceeded"; it works at depth 300. CE-1269 is the same kind of limit for a
sum whose body is nested about 2,000 levels deep.

## Cause and options

The lowering, the passes and the printer of the pipeline walk the expression
and the instructions by recursion, and a stack overflow is caught in one
place (`sliceError()` of `compilation/ir/adapter.ts`) and reported as a
decline. So the result is never a crash or an internal error, but the depth
at which the decline starts is the host's stack, not a property of the
expression.

(a) A stated depth limit, checked while lowering: an expression nested deeper
than a fixed number of levels declines on every host. Size S; the limit must
be below the smallest stack of a supported host. (b) Walks with an explicit
stack. Size L, and the engine's own walks (`.type`, canonicalization) have the
same limit, so the gain is small while those stay recursive.
