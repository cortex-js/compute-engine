In Epsil, an error in a subtraction whose first operand has a unary minus underlines that operand, not the operand that made the error.

## Witness

At commit `949ad8a3266435d8c7fd71844cbb69b88a10225a`:

```
let s: any = "a"
let y = 5
let v = -(y + 2) - s
frames     ->  Negate argument 1, Add argument 1
underline  ->  y + 2      expected: s, or the whole subtraction
```

## Cause and options

The canonical form of `a - b` is `Add(a, Negate(b))`, so the error of `s`
has the frame `Negate argument 1`. `locateError()`
(`src/epsil/error-location.ts`) finds one written `Negate`, the unary minus
of `-(y + 2)`, takes it as the call of that frame, and selects its operand.
The written `Subtract` also makes a canonical `Negate` (of its second
operand), but the search does not count it.

Option: count a written `Subtract` as a possible call of a `Negate` frame,
whose argument is the second operand of the subtraction. Then the statement
has two candidates, and the walk or the wider fallback gives the location.
The `holders` filter of `frameArgument()` must then also count the second
operand of a written subtraction as a holder of a `Negate`.
