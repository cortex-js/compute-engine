An integral with a constant before a fraction that holds the differential, `\int_1^2 2\frac{dx}{x}`, gives a wrong value.

## Witness

Readings of 2026-11-06, at commit `23db78e092dd67fa2ec95c360ee73dbf35e1b1ea`:

```
\int_1^2 2\frac{dx}{x}   parses to Integrate(2·(d·x)/x, Limits(d, 1, 2))
                         evaluate() gives 3; the value is 2 ln 2 ≈ 1.386
\int 2\frac{dx}{x}       evaluate() gives d^2; the value is 2 ln|x|
\int_1^2 \frac{2dx}{x}   gives 2 ln 2 (correct)
```

## Cause and options

`parseSubintegrand()` in `latex-syntax/dictionary/definitions-calculus.ts`
looks for the differential only at the end of a product, in the numerator
of a fraction that is the whole body, and in the one argument of a
function. In `2\frac{dx}{x}` (raw parse
`InvisibleOperator(2, Divide(InvisibleOperator(d, x), x))`) the fraction is
the last factor of a product, and the search does not go into it. With no
differential found, the canonical form takes the free symbol `d` as the
integration variable. Fix: when the last factor of a product is a fraction,
search its numerator, as the `Divide` case does.
