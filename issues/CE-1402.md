The result of `parse(…)` given directly as an argument is not evaluated: `evaluate(parse(#"1011_2 \cdot 101_2"#))` is a product of two `BaseForm` and not 55.

## Witness

Epsil CLI, main 412113c86:

- `evaluate(parse(#"1011_2 \cdot 101_2"#))` gives
  `BaseForm(11, 2) * BaseForm(5, 2)`; `let p = parse(…); p` gives 55.
- `solve(parse("161_b + 134_b = 315_b"), b)` stays
  `Solve(Parse(…), b)`; through a `let` it gives `[0, 8]`.

Workaround: bind the parsed expression with `let` first.

## Cause and options

The expression that `Parse` returns is not evaluated again by the operator
that receives it (`Evaluate` evaluates `Parse(…)` once, to the parsed form;
`Solve` holds its operand and sees `Parse`). Fix: `Parse` evaluates to the
evaluated parsed expression where its caller evaluates its operand, and a
holding operator such as `Solve` evaluates a `Parse` operand first. Check the
documented contract of `Parse` before choosing.
