A numeric integral of a narrow peak at a bound gives exactly 0 with no error.

## Witness

```
\int_0^1 e^{-10^8 x} dx                 .N() gives 0, should be 1e-8
\int_0^1 \frac{e^{-10^8 x}}{x^{0.99}} dx  .N() gives 0, should be 82.704
```

With the rate `10^6`, the second integral gives `86.52 ± 0.12` (value
`86.602`). `evaluate()` of the first gives the exact value.

## Cause and options

`adaptiveQuadrature` (`numerics/gauss-kronrod.ts`) starts with 16 panels. The
node nearest to `0` is at about `2.7e-4`, where `e^{-10^8 x}` underflows, so
every node gives `0`, the Gauss and Kronrod sums agree, and the result is `0`,
converged, with an error of `0`. `hiddenEdgeError` does not sample a bound of
the integral, because the integrand can be singular there. Fix: sample at
`a + (b − a)·2^(−k)` toward each bound, and bisect the first panel toward the
bound while these samples differ from the node level. Also possible: when the
quadrature gives `0`, use the antiderivative.
