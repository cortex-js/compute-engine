Two compile tests fail on main: they expect a point times or divided by a list to give no shader code, and it now gives correct code.

## Witness

`npx jest test/compute-engine/compile-restricted-point.test.ts` at
`1610c044ab98d71f2bd8569442cc88565f7cbaa2` (CE-1569): 2 failed, 41 passed.
The failing cases are "a point times a list fails closed on the shader
targets" (`Multiply(Tuple(x, 1), List(1, 2))`) and "a point divided by a list
fails closed on the shader targets" (`Divide(Tuple(3, 1), List(1, 2))`), at
`compile-restricted-point.test.ts:205`.

The interpreter gives a list of points for both:
`[(x, 1), (2x, 2)]` and `[(3, 1), (3/2, 1/2)]`. GLSL now compiles them to
`vec2[2](vec2(x * 1.0, v * 1.0), vec2(x * 2.0, v * 2.0))` and
`vec2[2](vec2(3.0, 1.0), vec2(1.5, 0.5))`, the same values. JavaScript gives
`[[2, 1], [4, 2]]` at `x = 2` and `[[3, 1], [1.5, 0.5]]`.

## Cause and options

CE-1569 made a point times a list of numbers compile on GLSL as an array of
points (`ir-glsl-point-arrays.test.ts` was updated) but did not update the two
cases of `compile-restricted-point.test.ts` with a literal point. The test
comment also says that a point divided by a list stays unevaluated in the
interpreter, which is no longer true. Fix: move the two cases out of the
"fails closed" table into a test that the compiled GLSL succeeds, and correct
the comment. The two cases with `P = PointList(t, 1)` (an undecided component
type) still decline and stay. Size S.
