A finite double integral with two different divisors that are zero at the same point gives a value with an uncertainty of about 10¹¹.

## Witness

```
\int_0^1\int_0^1 \frac{1}{\sqrt{(x-0.5)^2+(y-0.5)^2}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{(x-0.5)^2+4(y-0.5)^2}} dy\,dx
  .N() gives 8.3376126 ± 143915964629
```

Both terms grow like `1/r` near `(0.5, 0.5)`, so the integral is finite. The
witness is `crafted`: two different quadratic divisors with the same zero
were chosen to reach the case.

## Cause and options

`integrateAroundSingularPoint` (`library/calculus.ts`) cuts the box at each
singular point and shifts the variables to the point, with its divisor
replaced by the exact quadratic form. It replaces one divisor per point. When
a second divisor is zero at the same point, it keeps the rounding error of
the shift at the corner, so the function returns `undefined`, and the whole
box is integrated by the nested quadrature, which cannot resolve the point.

1. Replace every divisor that is zero at the point by its exact quadratic
   form (keep a list of forms for each point).
2. Keep the decline, and give `NaN` when the error of the nested quadrature
   is much larger than the estimate.
