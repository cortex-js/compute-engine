The fractional part of a large multiple of π evaluates to a wrong number: `evaluate()` gives `2643000000` for `Fract(10^30·π)`, whose value is about `0.503`.

## Witness

At `fdbbf5091`:

| Program | The engine gives | The value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ce.box(['Fract', ['Multiply', 'Pi', ['Power', 10, 30]]]).N()` | `0.502884197169399375106` | right |
| the same, `.evaluate().N()`, precision `auto` | `2643000000` | about `0.5029` |
| the same, `.evaluate().N()`, `ce.precision = 'machine'` | `0` | about `0.5029` |
| `ce.box(['Add', 1.5, ['Fract', ['Multiply', 'Pi', ['Power', 10, 30]]]]).evaluate()`, precision `auto` | `2643000001.5` | about `2.0029` |
| the same, precision `machine` | `0` | about `2.0029` |

The value of `Fract(10^30·π)` is `0.50288419716939937510…` (the digits of π
after the 31st). Both JavaScript compilers, with and without the profile
`numeric-kernel`, write the literal `0.5028841971693994`. The random tester
(`scripts/js-profile-fuzz.ts`) reports the shape
`Fract(Multiply(_, Power(_, _)))` as a disagreement with the interpreter
(33 programs of seed 171).

## Cause (not confirmed)

`evaluate()` gives the exact form `10^30·π − 3141592653589793238462643383279`
and its numeric value is computed with fewer digits than the subtraction of
two numbers of about `3·10^30` needs. `.N()` of the unevaluated node is
right, so the fault is in the exact rewrite of `Fract`: it should give the
numeric value, or keep `Fract(…)` when the exact difference cannot be
evaluated with enough digits.
