A derivative, an integral or a limit with no variable written, made before a variable of its formula gets a formula as its value, works with respect to that variable instead of the variable of its formula.

## Witness

```js
const ce = new ComputeEngine();
const integral = ce.parse('\\int_0^1 G + x');
const nd = ce.expr(['ND', ['Multiply', 2, 'G'], 1]);
ce.assign('G', ce.parse('\\sin(x)'));
integral.evaluate();   // x + 1/2       (must be 3/2 − cos(1))
nd.evaluate();         // 2             (must be 2cos(1) ≈ 1.0806)
```

Parsed after the assignment, the two give `3/2 − cos(1)` and `1.0806`
(CE-1787). A lone symbol (`\int_0^1 G`, `ND(G, 1)`) gives the same value in
the two orders (CE-1772), because its formula is read at evaluation.

## Cause and options

The canonical form chooses the variable when the expression is boxed
(`canonicalImplicitLimitFunction()`, `implicitLiteralParameters()`,
`library/calculus.ts`). At that time `G` has no value, so it is a candidate
and comes before `x` in sorted order: the literal is `G ↦ G + x`. The
literal does not record that its parameter was chosen and not written, so
the evaluation cannot choose again.

## Why the board must choose (cebatch-1829, 2026-11-26)

The witness reproduces at 4fa47666e1cb2bbe117c0939b57abb1b38c51928, and
`NIntegrate(2G, 0, 1)` boxed before the assignment gives `1` where it gives
`0.9194` boxed after it. No code changed. Two facts stop a fix without a
decision:

- The present value is a documented contract. `docs/SCOPING-MODEL.md` says:
  "an implicit form that is boxed before `G := sin(x)` and is not a bare
  symbol keeps the variable `G`".
- For `Limit`, `ND`, `NLimit` and `NIntegrate`, the canonical form of the
  implicit form is the canonical form of the explicit one. Boxed before the
  assignment, `Limit(G + 1, 0)` and `\lim_{G \to 0} (G + 1)` are both
  `Limit(Function(Block(G + 1), G), 0)`. A user who writes the variable `G`
  must keep it, so the evaluation cannot choose again for these four
  operators unless the canonical form changes. Only `Integrate` has a mark:
  `Limits(Nothing, 0, 1)`, where `\int_0^1 (G + x) dG` gives
  `Limits(G, 0, 1)`.

The options, each with the witness `\int_0^1 G + x` and `ND(2G, 1)`, boxed
before `G := sin(x)`:

- (a) The implicit forms keep the body as written and choose the variable at
  evaluation, as the lone symbol does. The integral gives `3/2 − cos(1)` and
  `ND` gives `1.0806`. The canonical form of the five operators with no
  variable written changes: `ND(2G, 1)` stays `ND(2G, 1)`, where it is
  `ND(Function(Block(2G), G), 1)` today. Each reader of that form changes
  with it: the free-variable walk (`implicitIntegralVariables()`), `subs()`,
  the type handlers, the serializers and the compilation of the five
  operators. Size L.
- (b) Only `Integrate`. When every limit is `Limits(Nothing, …)` and a
  parameter of the literal holds a formula at evaluation, the evaluation
  makes the literal again from the body. The integral gives `3/2 − cos(1)`;
  `ND` still gives `2`. This covers the one implicit form that LaTeX can
  write (`\int_a^b f` with no `dx`); the other four come from MathJSON and
  Epsil only. `Integrate(Function(G + x, G), Limits(Nothing, 0, 1))` written
  by hand changes too, but it is the canonical form of the same LaTeX.
  `unknowns`, `freeVariables`, `subs()` and the compilation of the boxed
  integral read the literal and must make the same choice, or they disagree
  with the value: today they say `x` is free. Size M with these readers, and
  the rule in `docs/SCOPING-MODEL.md` then differs between `Integrate` and
  the other four operators.
- (c) Recommended. Keep the documented limitation and close the issue. The
  integral gives `x + 1/2` and `ND` gives `2`, as today. The workaround is to
  write the variable (`\int_0^1 (G + x) dx`, `ND(x ↦ 2G, 1)`, which read the
  formula of `G` at evaluation), or to box the expression after the
  assignment. The limitation is in the doc comment of
  `implicitVariableCandidates()` and in `docs/SCOPING-MODEL.md` already. The
  reason for (c): the case needs an integral with no `dx` over two symbols,
  one of which gets a formula later, in a host that keeps the boxed
  expression across the assignment. Whether Tycho keeps boxed expressions
  across an assignment is not verified.
