A convergent integral from a number larger than about 10¹³ to infinity gives `NaN` when it is computed numerically.

## Witness

```
\int_{10^{14}}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{1.5}}  .N()   gives NaN, exact 2·10⁻⁷
\int_{10^{14}}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x(\ln x)^2} .N() gives NaN, exact 0.0310210
```

Readings of 2026-11-07, after the CE-1041 change. The bound 10¹² gives the
value.

## Cause and options

`quadrature` (`numerics/gauss-kronrod.ts`) computes a `divergent` result over
`[c, ∞)` again in two parts, split at `c + 1`. The far part uses
x = c + (1 − s)/s, and its integrand is about `f(c)/s²` for s > 1/c: its
shells decrease as 1/s² for log₂(c) bisections, and the divergence test
reports a divergence before it reaches s = 1/c.

Option: split at `c + min(|c|, 2⁴⁰)` and keep the map of the near part. A
scaled near part can miss a peak of width 1 next to `c`.
