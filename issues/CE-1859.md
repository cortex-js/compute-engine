A minimal normal form of a boolean expression with 10 variables takes about 17 seconds, and one with 12 variables, which the functions still accept, would take minutes.

## Witness

`MinimalDNF` of the DNF of a random truth table with about half of its rows
true, the mean of 3 calls on this machine (measured at
c08983cf83416188fbd555e2c69d8f79675658d7, script
`scratchpad/bench-dnf.mts` of the session, not in the repo):

```
6 variables:     85 ms
8 variables:  1,043 ms
9 variables:  3,991 ms
10 variables: 17,264 ms
```

The time grows about 4 times per variable. The functions refuse an
expression of more than 12 variables (`logic-analysis.ts`, "Maximum 12
variables"), so 11 and 12 variables are accepted, at an estimated 1 and 5
minutes, during which the host does not respond. The time was the same
before and after the cover search of CE-1858: it is spent in the
computation of the prime implicants, not in the cover.

## Cause and options

The prime implicants are computed by Quine-McCluskey in
`src/compute-engine/library/logic-analysis.ts`; `MinimalCNF`,
`PrimeImplicants` and `PrimeImplicates` share it. Not investigated further.

- (a) Recommended: profile the combine step (each round compares terms
  pairwise; grouping the terms by their number of ones and keying them
  as bit masks makes a round about linear in the number of terms), and
  measure again at 10 and 12 variables.
- (b) Lower the limit to the number of variables that stays within a host
  budget (8 variables, about 1 second), so a larger expression stays
  unevaluated.
