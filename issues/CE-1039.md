The derivative of an indefinite integral with respect to its own variable stays unevaluated.

## Witness

At commit `ffcfe2b5d3d67556d2bb64fd7ae2a9ec5e084056`:

```
\frac{d}{dx}\int x^2\,dx    evaluate gives D(int(x^2 dx), x), should be x^2
```

## Cause and options

The derivative rules have no rule for an `Integrate` whose `Limits` clause
has no bounds. `differentiateIntegral()` in
`src/compute-engine/symbolic/derivative.ts` declines it on purpose: it
handles definite integrals only. Options: (a) the fundamental theorem of
calculus: the derivative of `∫ f dx` with respect to `x` is `f`, and with
respect to another variable `y` it is `∫ ∂f/∂y dx` (equal up to a constant
of integration, which an antiderivative leaves open); (b) evaluate the
antiderivative first, then differentiate it, which fails when there is no
closed form. (a) is the smaller and more general change. Size S.
